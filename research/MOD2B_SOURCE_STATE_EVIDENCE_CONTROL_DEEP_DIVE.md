# MOD 2B DEEP DIVE — CHALLENGING SOURCE-STATE MONOPOLY OVER NUCLEAR-LEGACY EVIDENCE

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Bloc replacement topic:**
**“Evaluating whether source States should retain exclusive authority to classify, disclose, and assess evidence of transboundary contamination from their nuclear-testing and production legacies”**

**Speaking time:** 75 seconds
**Status:** Research, attack map, bloc differentiation and solution architecture only — **NO SPEECH DRAFTED**

---

# 0. EXECUTIVE VERDICT

This topic is aggressive because it asks one question testing and production States do not want framed publicly:

> **Can the State that created the risk control the records, choose the model, assess the harm and then declare its own response sufficient?**

Japan’s answer should not be “source States lose all authority.” That is legally and politically unsustainable.

Japan’s winning position is:

> **The source State retains custody of classified information and the territorial State retains consent over site access—but neither should remain the sole authority judging disputed transboundary harm.**

### Recommended scope decisions

1. **“Production legacies” means nuclear-weapons production**, not routine safeguarded civilian production. This protects Japan, India, Iran, UAE and France from civilian-scope drift.
2. **Successor record custodians are included.** A State controlling a predecessor’s archives has an evidentiary cooperation responsibility, without automatically inheriting every legal liability.
3. **No automatic liability.** Disclosure, assessment or technical assistance does not constitute admission of legal responsibility.
4. **No compulsory site access.** The territorial State controls consent. The source or archive State should not use territorial consent as an excuse to withhold records located in its own archives.
5. **No blanket declassification.** Weapon design, intelligence methods and operational vulnerabilities remain protected. Environmental conclusions, dose methodology and non-sensitive source data should be maximized.

---

# 1. DEFINITIONS — CONTROL THE VOCABULARY

## Source State

The State whose government conducted the nuclear test or controlled the weapons-production activity that generated the legacy.

## Territorial State

The State on whose territory the site is currently located. It controls consent to physical access under sovereignty.

## Successor record custodian

A State that controls archives, technical data or institutional records inherited from a predecessor State. Record custody creates an evidentiary cooperation question; it does **not automatically settle successor liability**.

## Affected State

A State claiming or investigating environmental or human consequences from a plausible transboundary pathway.

## Affected community

People whose health, land, water, food systems, culture or displacement may be linked to the legacy. Communities are evidence holders, not merely aid recipients.

## Classification authority

The national body deciding whether a record remains secret. Classification is not the same as scientific validity.

## Independent review

A technical process involving agreed experts or laboratories that can test methodology and conclusions without automatically receiving unrestricted access to classified documents or sites.

# 2. THE LEGAL AND POLITICAL BASE

## UNGA Resolution 79/60 gives Japan direct language

The Assembly:

- encourages cooperation to assist victims and assess/remediate contaminated environments;
- urges States that used or tested nuclear weapons to share, **as appropriate**, technical and scientific information regarding humanitarian and environmental consequences with affected States;
- recognizes that responsibilities to address harms lie with States that detonated, used or tested nuclear weapons, **subject to international law**.

### Meaning

The Assembly has already rejected absolute source-State silence. The unresolved issue is how “as appropriate” is interpreted and who reviews disputed evidence.

## TPNW Articles 6 and 7

For parties:

- Article 6 requires victim assistance and environmental remediation in areas under jurisdiction/control;
- Article 7 requires cooperation and assistance;
- Article 7(6) places a special assistance responsibility on a party that used or tested nuclear weapons.

### Gap

No nuclear-armed State is party, so the strongest positive-obligation framework does not bind the principal source States.

## OHCHR Marshall Islands report

The 2024 OHCHR report A/HRC/57/77 addressed human-rights barriers created by the Marshall Islands nuclear legacy. UN coverage identified information gaps as the most prevalent issue raised in consultations.

### Winning use

Access to evidence is not merely a scientific preference. It affects health, remedy, displacement, cultural rights and the ability to prove a claim.

## IAEA / CTBTO limits

- IAEA can assess radiological conditions and provide assistance when requested and within mandate.
- CTBTO systems detect test events and radionuclides but are not a universal legacy-remediation archive or tribunal.
- Neither automatically overrides territorial consent or national classification.

# 3. WHY EXCLUSIVE SOURCE-STATE CONTROL FAILS

## Failure 1 — Circular proof burden

1. Source State controls original records.
2. Affected State is told to prove exposure.
3. Source State withholds or summarizes records.
4. Lack of evidence is then cited as lack of harm.

### Aggressive principle

> **An evidence deficit created by the record-holder cannot be treated as affirmative evidence of safety.**

Do not call it a legal presumption of guilt. Call it a reason for independent assessment and priority access.

## Failure 2 — Model monopoly

Dose and plume reconstruction depend on:

- source term;
- yield and device characteristics;
- burst height or depth;
- meteorology;
- deposition;
- diet and behaviour assumptions;
- uncertainty treatment.

If one State controls inputs and model choices, affected States cannot reproduce the result.

## Failure 3 — Classification survives the security need

Some records remain genuinely sensitive. But “military origin” does not automatically mean every environmental measurement remains dangerous to disclose decades later.

The State should identify the **category of security harm**, not reveal the secret itself.

## Failure 4 — National assessment becomes final judgment

A national laboratory can be technically excellent and still face institutional conflict when its government’s legal or financial exposure depends on the conclusion.

Independent replication strengthens credible national findings; it need not insult them.

## Failure 5 — Communities become objects, not evidence holders

Official archives may omit:

- oral histories;
- local food pathways;
- displacement patterns;
- informal labour;
- cultural use of restricted land;
- unrecorded health impacts.

Community evidence should inform sampling and remediation indicators while remaining distinguishable from measured dose data.

# 4. CASE STUDIES — WHAT EACH PROVES

## Case A — Marshall Islands: information gaps obstruct remedy

### Verified base

- 67 US tests, 1946–1958;
- continuing displacement and environmental consequences;
- OHCHR report on human-rights barriers;
- US GAO/DOE monitoring and extensive public reporting exist, but disagreements with the Marshall Islands remain over risk and responsibility.

### Debate lesson

The United States is comparatively open and can be a constructive ally. Yet national reporting alone did not eliminate mistrust.

### Japan use

Demand joint data governance and independent review—not an anti-US accusation.

## Case B — Lucky Dragon: source boundaries failed

- vessel was well outside the declared danger area;
- all 23 crew were irradiated;
- source prediction and declared boundary did not contain fallout.

### Debate lesson

Affected States need access to source assumptions and warning methodology, not only the final boundary drawn by the testing State.

## Case C — Kazakhstan: archives are part of remediation

- 456 tests at the former Soviet site;
- IAEA assessment used information from Russian and Kazakh sources;
- archival data are essential for retrospective dosimetry;
- fallout measurements were recorded outside the test-site territory for multiple atmospheric events.

### Debate lesson

The territorial State can consent to access, but predecessor/successor records may remain elsewhere. Physical sovereignty and archive custody are separate.

## Case D — French Polynesia: declassification can change scientific conclusions

Peer-reviewed reassessment using declassified French documents and atmospheric transport modelling argued that earlier dose reconstructions underestimated exposure for important tests.

### Debate lesson

France should not be framed as irredeemably opaque. Its declassification made stronger independent science possible. France can lead a protected-review model while defending legitimate military secrecy and its compensation framework.

## Case E — United Kingdom testing records: contested access

UK veterans and families have publicly contested the completeness and accessibility of medical and testing records. The UK Government has stated that records are not improperly withheld and has reviewed material for release with privacy/security redactions.

### Debate lesson

State the dispute, not the allegation as settled fact. The policy solution is an indexed archive, privacy redaction and independent review of non-sensitive findings.

## Case F — China, Lop Nur

China conducted 45 tests at Lop Nur. Publicly available independent environmental and health information is limited relative to the scale of the programme.

### Safe question

> Will China support an indexed environmental archive and protected independent review without disclosing weapon designs?

### Never say

- China caused a specific cross-border disease without evidence.
- Contested 2020 testing allegations are proven. CTBTO said available evidence did not permit confidence about cause.

## Case G — DPRK, Punggye-ri

- six confirmed tests;
- site access denied;
- seismic and satellite evidence exists;
- cross-border environmental claims remain difficult to independently verify.

### Debate lesson

External monitoring can identify events, but denied access and absent source records prevent full environmental assessment.

## Case H — South Africa: protected verification can coexist with security

South Africa dismantled six weapons and accepted IAEA verification of programme termination. It demonstrates that security-sensitive transitions can be reviewed without publishing weapon blueprints.

# 5. JAPAN’S SOLUTION — THE THREE-KEY LEGACY EVIDENCE STANDARD

## Key 1 — Source Record Package

The source State or successor custodian provides, where available:

- indexed list of relevant records;
- declassified meteorological and plume data;
- environmental sampling and calibration information;
- dose-reconstruction methodology;
- broad release and waste categories;
- remediation history;
- reasoned classification categories for withheld material.

An index can identify that a record exists without revealing its contents.

## Key 2 — Affected-State Counter-Assessment

The affected or territorial State may:

- nominate national experts and community representatives;
- identify local exposure pathways;
- submit independent measurements;
- challenge assumptions;
- request clarification;
- consent to any site access.

## Key 3 — Independent Technical Review

A jointly agreed panel or laboratory consortium:

- reviews methodology;
- reproduces calculations where possible;
- separates measured data from models and testimony;
- records unresolved uncertainty;
- issues public findings and a protected annex where required.

### Decision rule

No party receives a unilateral veto over the scientific conclusion. If consensus fails, the report publishes:

1. agreed facts;
2. disputed assumptions;
3. each model’s outcome;
4. data gaps;
5. recommended additional evidence.

# 6. CLASSIFICATION WITHOUT EVIDENCE MONOPOLY

## Four information levels

### GREEN — public

- sampling results;
- dose ranges and uncertainty;
- environmental pathways;
- broad remediation status;
- health-support eligibility criteria;
- record index.

### AMBER — protected expert access

- detailed source-term ranges;
- high-resolution site maps;
- personnel identifiers;
- sensitive facility process data.

### RED — withheld operational/design information

- weapon design;
- intelligence sources/methods;
- exploitable security vulnerabilities;
- current operational locations;
- command-and-control.

### BLACK — periodic classification review

Material not releasable now receives a review date and a reason category. “Military origin” alone should not make secrecy perpetual.

---

# 7. LIABILITY-NEUTRAL COOPERATION

To keep USA, France, UK, India and other security-focused States in the room:

- disclosure is not an admission of legal liability;
- technical review does not determine compensation;
- assistance may begin before attribution is settled;
- territorial consent remains mandatory for site access;
- record sharing is “as appropriate” and security-filtered;
- successor archive custody is not automatic successor guilt.

### Aggressive line with legal protection

> **Japan is not asking States to confess. Japan is asking them to produce the evidence on which their assurances depend.**

# 10. AGGRESSIVE POINT BANK

1. **No data is not evidence of no harm.**
2. **A classified input cannot produce an unquestionable public conclusion.**
3. **If a safety finding is robust, independent replication strengthens it.**
4. **The affected State cannot be required to disprove a model it is not allowed to inspect.**
5. **Territorial consent governs site access; it does not govern records held in another State’s archive.**
6. **Successor custody creates an evidence question before it creates a liability question.**
7. **Classification must identify a security harm, not merely a military origin.**
8. **A record index reveals existence, not weapon design.**
9. **Assistance can begin before attribution; uncertainty should not suspend healthcare or basic monitoring.**
10. **Source States deserve a voice, not a monopoly.**

# 12. EXPECTED ATTACKS AND COUNTERS

## “This violates sovereignty.”

**Counter:** Site access is consent-based. Archive sharing concerns records held by the source/custodian. Independent review can be remote and protected.

## “This exposes national-security information.”

**Counter:** Green/Amber/Red classification protects design and vulnerabilities. Source State must identify the category of harm, not reveal the secret.

## “Source laboratories are the most technically qualified.”

**Counter:** Keep them in the review. Expertise justifies participation, not exclusivity.

## “No liability has been established.”

**Counter:** The mechanism explicitly separates evidence cooperation from liability determination.

## “Affected-community testimony is not scientific data.”

**Counter:** Correct; label it separately. It identifies pathways and sampling priorities but does not replace measured dose.

## “This is anti-France / anti-US.”

**Counter:** The mechanism rewards declassification, GAO audit and protected review. It is harshest on complete opacity, not any named alliance.

## “Japan is hiding Fukushima.”

**Counter:** The topic is weapons testing and weapons production. Routine civilian operations are technically and legally distinct. Japan accepts international monitoring in the civilian file as well.

## FINAL RESEARCH CONCLUSION

The aggressive position is not “source States are liars.” That is crude and easy to reject.

The winning position is:

> **Source States control legitimate secrets. Territorial States control access. Affected States control neither—and therefore need a guaranteed role in the evidence. No single actor should control all three keys.**

Japan becomes the delegation that turns accusation into an evidentiary constitution.
