## PART C — THE STRATEGIC READ

**1. The arithmetic on THIS topic is overwhelming:** ~22-24 delegates lean YES on monitoring + remediation; only 4 hard NOs (France, UK, Russia, DPRK); ~6 abstain (China, US, Israel, India + swing). **No rival can block a monitoring/remediation clause.**

**2. Kazakhstan is your accidental ally:** she left the bloc but her Semipalatinsk testimony is the single most powerful weapon on this topic, and she *will* speak on it. Don't recruit her back — just coordinate with her delegate on this specific caucus. Two legacy voices (hers + yours) = the moral floor.

**3. The adversary math:** France/UK/Russia/DPRK can't win a vote on this — their only play is to poison the *language* (liability, TPNW references, enforcement). That's exactly why the Barbados liability fix + the "for their respective States parties" formula matter: keep the text voluntary and the four flags can only abstain, not rally opposition.

**4. Germany + Norway are secretly your best bridge-recruits ON THIS TOPIC:** they're NATO/umbrella states who voted yes on the legacy file and attend TPNW meetings as observers. On a transboundary-contamination clause, they can be pulled to co-sponsor — that's the P3-tolerance proof the room needs.

**5. The US vulnerability (Runit Dome) is real but don't attack her on it publicly in the opener** — a "Runit Dome" attack in a caucus speech hands China the ALPS weapon to use on you. Use Runit as the *background fact* and let the room draw the conclusion; reserve the direct strike for a POI or a later slot if the US attacks you first.
