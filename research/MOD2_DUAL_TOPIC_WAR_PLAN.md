# 1. THE TWO POSSIBLE TOPICS

## Topic A — selected committee topic

> **Deliberation on existing loopholes in nuclear treaties**

### Japan’s interpretation

Use the legally stronger phrase **“coverage, verification and implementation gaps in the nuclear-treaty architecture.”**

Do not spend 75 seconds listing treaty names.

### Japan’s top surprise point

> The Joint Convention on radioactive waste normally excludes military/defence waste unless a State declares it or permanently transfers it into exclusively civilian management.

Few delegates will find this.

---

## Topic B — bloc replacement topic

> **Evaluating whether source States should retain exclusive authority to classify, disclose, and assess evidence of transboundary contamination from their nuclear-testing and production legacies**

### Short Chair version

> **Challenging exclusive source-State control over evidence of transboundary nuclear legacies**

### Scope interpretation

- weapons testing and weapons-production legacies only;
- successor archive custodians included;
- no automatic liability;
- host-State consent for site access;
- protected review for sensitive records.

# 3. MOTION AND VOTING EXECUTION

## Exact motion

> “The Delegation of Japan motions for a moderated caucus on evaluating whether source States should retain exclusive authority to classify, disclose, and assess evidence of transboundary contamination from their nuclear-testing and weapons-production legacies, with a total duration of ten minutes and an individual speaking time of seventy-five seconds.”

### Why “weapons-production” is inserted orally

It blocks Fukushima and routine civilian production from scope without advertising a Japan-specific defence.

## Coordination

1. Only Japan raises the exact flagship wording.
2. Mexico and Kazakhstan support it immediately if the rules permit verbal support.
3. If competing motions are entertained, bloc members do **not** propose slightly different wording and split votes.
4. Every bloc member votes for the identical motion.
5. Keep the Three-Key solution unrevealed until Japan’s intervention.

## If the Chair asks for simplification

Use:

> **“Source-State control over evidence of transboundary nuclear-testing and weapons-production legacies.”**

Do not surrender “source-State control”; that is what creates debate.

# 5. JAPAN’S HIDDEN ADVANTAGE

## Public appearance

Japan appears to defend affected States, scientific credibility and legitimate security classification equally.

## Actual strategic benefit

- source scope is weapons-only, shielding routine civilian Japanese activity;
- Japan has no weapons-testing legacy to defend;
- Lucky Dragon gives Japan affected-State standing;
- USA is the comparatively transparent source State and does not become the main villain;
- France receives a constructive lane and avoids bloc fracture;
- India receives consent, universality and security protection;
- Japan owns the Three-Key solution that the topic logically demands;
- main pressure shifts to record-control and denied-access problems in Russia, China, DPRK, UK and Israel.

## Do not reveal

- that weapons-only scope was selected to block Fukushima attacks;
- that France has a preassigned constructive defence;
- that Japan will unveil the Three-Key Standard;
- bloc vote count or majority.

# 6. JAPAN’S RESEARCH REVEAL — THREE KEYS

This is not a speech. It is the mechanism Japan should unveil.

### KEY 1 — Source package

Indexed records, declassified environmental data, methodology, uncertainty and classification reasons.

### KEY 2 — Affected-State counter-assessment

National experts, community pathways, independent sampling and challenges to assumptions.

### KEY 3 — Independent technical review

Protected expert review, reproducible methods, public agreed facts and clearly identified disagreements.

### Core rule

> **No source State loses its legitimate secrets. No affected State is forced to accept an unreviewable conclusion.**

# 9. POINT-OF-ORDER SHIELDS

## Russia / Soviet liability

> Japan assigns no automatic successor liability. It asks the custodian of relevant archives to cooperate in evidence review.

## France / independent reassessment

> Japan distinguishes France’s official position from peer-reviewed reassessment enabled by declassified documents. It does not declare one legally conclusive.

## China / Lop Nur

> Japan states only the verified test count and requests environmental data; it alleges no unverified named cross-border harm.

## DPRK

> Japan states six confirmed tests and denied independent access; it does not assert a proven cross-border release.

## USA / Marshall Islands

> Japan recognizes substantial GAO and DOE disclosure while noting documented information gaps and continuing disagreements.

## Communities

> Testimony identifies pathways and priorities; it remains analytically separate from measured dose and model output.
