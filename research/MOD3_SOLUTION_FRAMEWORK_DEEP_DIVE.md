# 0. EXECUTIVE VERDICT

The committee does not need another list of harms. It needs to connect mechanisms that currently operate in separate legal and technical silos.

### Existing pieces

- CTBTO’s International Monitoring System detects nuclear explosions.
- IAEA’s Incident and Emergency Centre, USIE, IRMIS and RANET support emergency information exchange and assistance.
- UNSCEAR assesses radiation sources, exposures and effects.
- IAEA ENVIRONET and GSG-15 support environmental-remediation knowledge and planning.
- UNGA resolutions 79/60 and 80/56 establish political momentum for victim assistance and remediation.
- TPNW Articles 6 and 7 provide a positive-obligation model for parties.

### The missing link

No single existing mechanism creates a universal chain from:

> **DETECTION → VALIDATION → NOTIFICATION → TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE → REMEDIATION → PUBLIC OUTCOME REVIEW**

Japan’s solution is therefore not to replace existing institutions. It is to make them interoperable while respecting mandate, consent and security boundaries.

---

# 1. THE CORE DIAGNOSIS — WHY THE CURRENT SYSTEM FRAGMENTS

## Gap 1 — Detection without remediation

The CTBTO IMS is designed to detect nuclear explosions. It is not a universal contamination-remediation body.

## Gap 2 — Emergency response without legacy continuity

IAEA emergency systems can coordinate information and requested assistance during incidents and emergencies. Historical testing and weapons-production legacies are usually existing exposure situations, not active emergencies.

## Gap 3 — Science without operational authority

UNSCEAR provides independent scientific assessments. It does not set protection policy, run emergency response or administer cleanup.

## Gap 4 — Remediation knowledge without automatic access or financing

ENVIRONET shares expertise and GSG-15 provides remediation guidance, but participation, site access, project finance and implementation remain State-led.

## Gap 5 — Political resolutions without a permanent delivery chain

UNGA 79/60 encourages cooperation and information-sharing. UNGA 80/56 establishes the modalities for a 2026 international meeting. Neither by itself creates a standing global operational mechanism.

## Gap 6 — Vulnerable States may lack laboratories

A State can be affected by a plausible atmospheric, marine or food-chain pathway without possessing:

- radionuclide laboratories;
- calibrated field equipment;
- dispersion models;
- trained health physicists;
- funds for long-term sampling;
- access to source records.

### Aggressive conclusion

> **A system that can detect an explosion but cannot carry the evidence through warning, assistance and measurable cleanup is not complete.**

## CTBTO International Monitoring System

### Verified capability

- planned network: 321 stations and 16 radionuclide laboratories = 337 facilities;
- around 90% operational;

### Japan use

Use CTBTO data/expertise only through existing legal arrangements and authorized channels. Never claim the General Assembly can commandeer IMS data.

## IAEA Incident and Emergency Centre

### Verified capability

The IEC is the global focal point for international preparedness, communication and response to nuclear/radiological incidents and emergencies, regardless of accident, negligence or deliberate act.

### Tools

- **USIE:** secure real-time emergency communication;
- **IRMIS:** international radiation-monitoring information during emergencies;
- **RANET:** expert teams, equipment, sampling, dose assessment, medical advice, source recovery and technical assistance on request;
- **Assessment and Prognosis Tools:** technical situation assessment.

### Limit

- emergency focus;
- assistance is request-based;
- does not replace national responsibility;
- not automatically a standing test-legacy remediation programme.

## IAEA ENVIRONET and GSG-15

### ENVIRONET

- connects States and experts working on contaminated-site remediation;
- provides training, demonstrations, experience exchange and planning/management knowledge;
- addresses sites affected by uranium, defence programmes, accidents and poorly regulated activities.

### GSG-15

IAEA Safety Guide on remediation strategy and process for areas affected by past activities or events.

Core principles:

- national framework;
- site-specific assessment;
- justification and optimization;
- responsible party and financing arrangements;
- stakeholder/public involvement;
- remediation and post-remediation monitoring;
- graded, risk-based approach.

### Limit

A safety guide is not a compulsory global cleanup force. National authorities retain legal responsibility and site access control.

### Mandate

- scientific evaluation of sources of ionizing radiation;
- exposure, effects and risks to people and environment;
- independent scientific foundation for policy.

### Limit

UNSCEAR expressly does not set protection policy or emergency-planning rules.

### Correct role in Japan’s framework

- methods and reference science;
- independent evidence synthesis;
- no operational command.

Long-term global-effects science and policy evidence—not incident response or contaminated-site remediation.

# 3. JAPAN’S THREE-LAYER SOLUTION

# LAYER I — MONITOR

## A. Nuclear Radiological Data Interoperability Protocol

A voluntary common data schema, not a new inspection authority.

### Minimum fields

1. date/time and sampling period;
2. approximate location and geographic resolution;
3. radionuclide and physical/chemical form;
4. activity concentration and unit;
5. detection limit;
6. measurement uncertainty;
7. instrument and calibration standard;
8. sample chain of custody;
9. quality-control laboratory;
10. observed value versus modelled estimate;
11. environmental medium: air, water, soil, sediment, food or biota;
12. public/protected classification level.

### Why this matters

Delegations frequently compare:

- different units;
- different detection limits;
- measurements to models;
- single samples to time series;
- gross activity to radionuclide-specific readings.

A common schema prevents political misuse of incomparable data.

## B. Baseline nodes

Regional or national baseline datasets should be established before a suspected event so a later anomaly can be distinguished from background variation.

## C. Three evidence labels

- **MEASURED:** direct calibrated observation.
- **MODELLED:** calculated pathway/dose with assumptions stated.
- **UNRESOLVED:** insufficient evidence or conflicting results.

Never convert “unresolved” into “safe” or “harm proven.”

## D. Information tiers

- **Public:** environmental readings, uncertainty, protective guidance.
- **Protected expert:** high-resolution maps, detailed source terms, sensitive facility process data.
- **Classified:** weapon design, current operational location, command-and-control and exploitable vulnerability.

---

# LAYER II — RESPOND

## A. Notification threshold

Japan should not demand notification for any detectable radionuclide. Natural background and legitimate activity would create false alarms.

Recommended trigger:

> **Credible monitoring or modelling indicating a plausible transboundary pathway with potential radiological safety significance for another State.**

This borrows the design logic of the Early Notification Convention without falsely claiming that Convention covers every weapons-related event.

## B. Notification content

Without delay, share through the applicable channel:

- nature and time of the event/finding;
- approximate location;
- radionuclides detected or suspected;
- available environmental readings;
- plausible pathways;
- protective actions already taken;
- uncertainty and next update time;
- contact point for technical exchange.

## C. Notification before attribution

Notification does not require agreement on:

- who caused the event;
- legal liability;
- compensation;
- treaty violation;
- political intent.

### Winning doctrine

> **Warn first. Attribute later.**

## D. Joint Technical Review Cell

On request and consent:

- source State/national authority;
- affected State experts;
- agreed independent laboratory;
- relevant IAEA/CTBTO/UN expertise within mandate;
- published agreed facts and disputed assumptions.

## E. RANET pathway

If national capacity is exceeded, affected States can request:

- environmental sampling;
- radiation surveys;
- dose assessment;
- medical support;
- decontamination advice;
- source search/recovery;
- installation assessment.

Japan should expand participation and regional readiness, not invent a duplicate response network.

---

# LAYER III — REMEDIATE

## A. Request-based Nuclear Legacy Assistance Window

A voluntary coordination/finance window linked to existing UN and IAEA expertise.

### Three funding tracks

1. **Capacity grants:** equipment, training, laboratory quality assurance and baseline surveys.
2. **Assessment missions:** characterization, pathway modelling, health/environment baselines and feasibility studies.
3. **Multi-year remediation support:** containment, removal, access control, water/soil treatment and post-remediation monitoring.

## B. Priority criteria

Use a transparent score based on:

- credible transboundary pathway;
- radiological severity and persistence;
- population and ecosystem exposure;
- displacement/cultural impact;
- affected State’s capacity gap;
- urgency of stabilizing material;
- strength and uncertainty of evidence.

## C. Community participation

Affected communities help define:

- local food and water pathways;
- land use and cultural access;
- acceptable restrictions;
- health-support needs;
- remediation outcome indicators;
- safe-return conditions.

Community testimony informs sampling. It does not replace measurement.

## D. Outcome indicators

Funding is not success. Publish:

- contaminated material secured or removed;
- change in environmental concentration/dose trends;
- area under access restriction;
- water/soil/food compliance against chosen criteria;
- people receiving health support;
- cost and schedule performance;
- post-remediation monitoring results;
- unresolved risks.

## E. Financing safeguards

- voluntary contributions;
- source States encouraged, not automatically held liable;
- needs-based allocation;
- independent financial audit;
- no donor veto over scientific findings;
- multi-year commitments for long legacies;
- assistance does not prejudice legal claims.

# 5. DRAFT-READY SOLUTION PACKAGE

## Recommendation 1 — common data protocol

Requests the Secretary-General, through UNODA and in consultation with relevant international organizations acting strictly within existing mandates, to develop a voluntary Nuclear Radiological Data Interoperability Protocol.

## Recommendation 2 — notification and review

Encourages participating States to notify potentially affected States when credible evidence indicates a plausible transboundary pathway of radiological safety significance and to permit consent-based joint technical review.

## Recommendation 3 — RANET and laboratory capacity

Encourages expanded participation, exercises and registered capabilities under RANET, plus regional laboratory-quality and baseline-monitoring support.

## Recommendation 4 — remediation window

Invites voluntary technical and financial contributions to a request-based Nuclear Legacy Assistance Window supporting characterization, remediation and long-term monitoring.

## Recommendation 5 — public outcomes

Requests participating remediation projects to publish indicators, expenditure, timelines, uncertainty and post-remediation outcomes while protecting legitimate security information.

# 10. AGGRESSIVE SOLUTION POINT BANK

1. **Detection without notification is surveillance, not protection.**
2. **Notification after proven harm is warning after exposure.**
3. **A model without uncertainty is advocacy disguised as science.**
4. **Funding without outcome indicators is ceremony, not remediation.**
5. **Sovereignty protects control of territory; it does not justify silence about a plausible cross-border pathway.**
6. **Voluntary does not mean vague: name the trigger, actor, data fields and review date.**
7. **No institution should be assigned a power it does not legally possess.**
8. **Do not build a new network where an existing network only needs access, capacity and connection.**
9. **Communities identify pathways; laboratories quantify them. Both are necessary, neither substitutes for the other.**
10. **If every body is responsible for one fragment, nobody is responsible for the chain.**

# 11. EXPECTED ATTACKS AND COUNTERS

## “This duplicates CTBTO.”

CTBTO detects nuclear explosions. Japan’s protocol standardizes environmental data and links detection to affected-State notification and remediation.

## “This exceeds IAEA mandate.”

Japan says “within existing mandates,” uses assistance on request and gives the General Assembly no inspection authority.

## “This violates sovereignty.”

Site access is consent-based; assistance is request-based; notification concerns plausible effects on other States.

## “Notification admits liability.”

The framework expressly separates notification, attribution and liability.

## “Any detection will cause panic.”

The trigger is not any detection. It is credible evidence of a plausible transboundary pathway with potential radiological safety significance.

## “Voluntary mechanisms are toothless.”

The framework uses common fields, deadlines/updates, published outcomes and independent audit. Compulsory powers the UNGA lacks would be performative.

## “Why not use TPNW Articles 6/7?”

Use them as a model. No nuclear-armed State is party, so the UNGA needs a parallel inclusive route.

## “Japan is hiding Fukushima.”

The MOD concerns weapons-related proliferation threats. Japan nevertheless supports IAEA/UNSCEAR evidence and international emergency mechanisms across civilian safety files.

## “Why should source States fund without liability?”

Contributions may be voluntary and liability-neutral. Assistance can begin while legal positions remain reserved.

- IMS will comprise 337 facilities; around 90% are operational.
- IEC is the global focal point for emergency communication and response coordination.
- RANET mobilizes registered national capabilities upon request.

## FINAL RESEARCH CONCLUSION

The best solution is not another institution. It is a **chain of custody for responsibility**:

> **Use CTBTO to detect. Use common data to validate. Use existing channels to notify. Use RANET to assist. Use ENVIRONET and GSG-15 to remediate. Use public indicators to prove the cleanup worked.**

Japan wins by making the existing machinery function as one system—without inventing powers, sacrificing security or waiting for another treaty to fail.
