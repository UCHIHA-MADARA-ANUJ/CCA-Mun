# MUN COMMAND CENTER — DELEGATE FIELD MANUAL

One-page-able distillation of the full 48-skill suite (Master + Core Protocol + Skills 01–47 + templates pack).
Use this as the in-committee brain; use the specialist `SKILL.md` files for deep work.

---

## 1. THE FIVE COMMANDMENTS

1. **Accuracy** — every changing claim verified, sourced, dated, scoped, honestly bounded. Zero invention.
2. **Authenticity** — fits YOUR country, YOUR committee, YOUR simulation date.
3. **Usability** — usable in 30 seconds, a caucus, a negotiation, or a drafting session.
4. **Leverage** — preparation that wins influence: framing, evidence, coalitions, feasible proposals.
5. **Diplomatic discipline** — attack policies, never delegates.

---

## 2. EVIDENCE DOCTRINE (non-negotiable)

### Labels — never blur them
- `[VERIFIED FACT]` — credible, relevant source.
- `[CONTEXTUAL FACT]` — stable background.
- `[LIKELY INFERENCE]` — reasonable conclusion, not official statement.
- `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]` — advised move/framing.
- `[CONTESTED CLAIM]` — disputed or methodology-dependent.
- `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE IN COMMITTEE]` — insufficient evidence.

### Source tiers
1. **Primary authority:** UN documents/Digital Library, treaty texts, official vote records, government/permanent-mission statements, official agencies (WHO, UNHCR, WFP, IAEA, World Bank, IMF…).
2. **High-quality reporting:** wires, established broadcasters, serious investigations — only to confirm developing events.
3. **Analysis/expertise:** peer-reviewed research, policy institutes — context and interpretations.
4. **Discovery only:** blogs, social media, unsourced infographics — never the foundation of a claim.

### Rules
- **Atomize:** split complex claims into separate facts; verify each.
- **Inspect:** open the actual document, not just the search snippet. Confirm title, institution, date, scope.
- **Conflict rule:** if credible sources disagree, report the disagreement — don't pick the convenient version.
- **Safe speaking:** never claim more than the source supports.
- **Grades:** A = primary/current/direct; B = secondary/dated; C = context only; D = unusable. No A/B → don't make it a central attack.

## 3. THE PREPARATION PIPELINE (from Research Planner)

1. **Context Card** — country, committee, exact agenda, type (regular/historical/crisis), simulation date, speech limit, RoP, background guide, time available, deliverable.
2. **Objective formula** — "Prepare [COUNTRY] in [COMMITTEE] to [DEFEND/LEAD/BLOCK/NEGOTIATE] [POSITION] on [AGENDA] using [EVIDENCE/ALLIANCES/PROPOSALS] within [TIME]."
3. **Question tree** — Layer A committee reality → B agenda reality → C country reality → D committee strategy. Every question must end in a use case.
4. **Eight research lanes** — country position; committee mandate; timeline/status; legal framework; data/impact; stakeholders/blocs; solution design; opposition/red team.
5. **Priority system** — P0 committee-critical > P1 high-value > P2 useful depth > P3 research trap. When time is short: finish P0 before reading P1.
6. **Evidence cards** — claim, label, source, date/period, scope, what it proves, what it does NOT prove, safe speaking line, committee use, risk.
7. **Research-to-action** — every fact must become a speech line, POI, rebuttal, offer, clause rationale, amendment, defense, or approach decision. Otherwise downgrade it.

### Time-boxes
- **30 min:** mandate → stance + red lines → 5 verified facts → 5 priority delegations → opening speech + 3 solutions → 2 defenses + 2 POIs.
- **2 hrs:** context/mandate 15m → country position 25m → timeline/law/data 30m → bloc+negotiation 20m → policy+clauses 20m → speech/rebuttal pack 10m.
- **Evening/multi-day:** all 8 lanes; Day 4 = mock debate + red team + final rapid pack. Start the clause bank early — never delay drafting behind research.

## 5. THE KEY FRAMEWORKS

### FAMMS — every serious proposal must pass
- **F**easible — actors, resources, political support exist.
- **A**uthority — the committee/actor has the mandate.
- **M**easurable — indicators or reporting exist.
- **M**oney — who funds it, credibly.
- **S**afeguards — sovereignty, consent, rights, security, data, capacity.

### Speech structures
- **20 seconds:** Frame (problem+principle) → Evidence (one sharp fact) → Position (support/reject) → Action (demand/offer/propose).
- **60 seconds:** Hook → country interest → evidence → critique of insufficient approaches → two-part solution → coalition invitation.
- **A speech fails if:** any country could give it; >2 unexplained data points; no concrete ask; unsupported accusation; ignores mandate; sounds like a report.

### Bloc map
Core allies → transactional allies (need a concession) → swing states (decide the vote) → soft opponents (mechanism dispute) → hard opponents (red lines). Re-classify agenda-by-agenda; one past vote ≠ permanent loyalty.

### Negotiation sequence
1. Secure core allies → 2. agree 2–3 non-negotiables → 3. recruit swing states with tangible clauses/safeguards → 4. keep soft opponents out of the rival bloc → 5. draft early → 6. trade secondary language for core protections → 7. protect sponsor ownership of lead clauses.

### Operative clause test
Who acts? What do they do? Under what authority? With what resources? By when? How is progress reviewed? What safeguards?

### Mandate-safe drafting
Name: responsible actor, mandate basis, implementation pathway, funding source, time frame, monitoring mechanism, sovereignty/consent/data safeguards, measurable outcome. If you can't name them → the proposal is incomplete.

### Pressure language bank
- "What enforcement mechanism makes this operational?"
- "We cannot support an obligation without a credible financing route."
- "This clause transfers responsibility without building capacity."
- "The current wording is too broad to produce accountable implementation."
- "Before endorsing this text, we require a realistic timetable and implementing actor."

### Crisis method (per update)
Confirmed events → uncertain reports → actors+incentives → immediate risks → decision window → assets/authority → second-order consequences → best action + fallback + communication line.
**Directive must specify:** objective, authority/actor, action sequence, resources, timeline, opsec/public comms choice, contingency, political/legal risk. Dramatic ≠ effective.

### Red-team quality gate (before anything is "committee-ready")
1. Can a skeptic verify every central claim? 2. Authentic to country/committee/date? 3. Inside mandate or correctly routed? 4. What makes a swing state reject it? 5. Strongest rival argument — answered? 6. Usable in available time?

### The six evaluation tests
A Fact · B Country · C Mandate · D Opposition (strongest objection answered?) · E Usability · F Leadership (does it lead, not just react?).

## 7. FAILURE MODES → FIXES (know your own bugs)

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| "We support dialogue and peace" | Name interest, red line, proposal, actor, evidence |
| Ten facts, no argument | Keep only evidence that changes a move |
| Fake precision | Verify or remove — never invent specificity |
| Attacking countries | Attack the policy flaw, explain consequence, offer repair |
| "Create an international fund" | Apply FAMMS: who, how much, managed by whom, oversight |
| Future knowledge in a historical committee | Rebuild within the simulation-date boundary |
| Committee demanding powers it lacks | Route through the correct organ/agency/recommendation |

## 9. AWARDS & CHAIR INTELLIGENCE (skill 41)

**What chairs actually score:** knowledge (rules, topics, country), diplomacy, procedure, leadership, constructiveness in caucuses, resolution work, consistency across sessions. Quality > quantity — one frame-shifting intervention beats five generic speeches.

- **Three rooms:** formal debate, unmod caucuses, the paper trail (working papers, DRs, position paper). Build a record in all three.
- **Persona:** researcher + mediator + early leader. Assert presence in session one; become the person consulted in unmods; anchor a draft at the end.
- **Chair management:** procedural accuracy, punctuality, conduct rules, never misstate a rule, never deride other delegates.
- **Keep a contribution ledger** (drafted / amended / bridged / moved / crisis wins per session) — empty session = fix next session.
- **Position paper is often an award GATE** — missing or off-format disqualifies at many conferences. Treat as mandatory.
- **Endgame:** close debate only with a winning count; credit allies by name in the final statement.

## 10. DELIVERY (skill 42)

- **Pace:** 120–140 wpm — slower than feels natural. **The pause is the emphasis:** one beat before the key word, one after. Max 1–2 emphasized words per 30 s.
- **Timing math:** 30 s ≈ 70–80 words; 45 s ≈ 105–120; 60 s ≈ 140–155; 90 s ≈ 210–230. Target ~10% under the limit.
- **Structure:** first 10 s scripted (hook + country + stance), last 10 s scripted (the ask), middle modular.
- **Body:** 3-second eye-contact sweeps; waist-up gestures; no fillers; decisive placard.
- **Nerves:** box breathing (4-4-4-4) before standing; one friendly face for the first 3 s; never invent a number live.
- **Q&A:** answer ≤15 s — direct answer → one verified fact → redirect.

## 11. FLOOR, AGENDA & VOTE ENDGAME (skill 43)

- **Agenda-setting is strategic:** push the topic where your bloc is strongest and freshest.
- **Speakers list:** get on early; never dump your whole position in speech one; yields = coalition signals.
- **Motion precedence:** more disruptive motions win — unmod > mod; longer > shorter; longer speaking time > shorter. Raise a more disruptive motion to bury a rival's.
- **Amendments:** friendly (adopted without vote) absorb opposition; unfriendly are weapons — test every offered amendment with "who benefits?"
- **Voting endgame:** whip count before closing debate (often needs 2/3); reorder DRs (yours first if ahead, last if split); division of the question to strip rival clauses; roll-call to freeze waffling states.
- **Live whip ledger** by delegation: yes / lean / undecided / lean no / no / absent — updated after every speech.

## 12. POSITION PAPER (skill 44)

- **Format:** header (committee/topic/country/delegate), 3 sections (background ≈25%, country policy ≈40%, solutions ≈35%), works cited; 1–1.5 pages, single-spaced, 12 pt, third person; cite ≥3 documents NOT in the background guide; no AI content.
- **Country policy is the heart:** official statements, voting records, domestic laws, why the issue matters to THIS country, red lines + negotiable areas.
- **Solutions = mechanisms:** who acts, under what authority, with what funding, by when, with what oversight (FAMMS).
- **After submission:** extract a one-page podium cheat sheet; align opening speech with the paper's framing.

## 13. CRISIS BACKROOM (skill 45)

- **Five instruments:** committee directive (voted), personal directive (portfolio powers, not voted), crisis note (private letter), JPD (joint powers), communiqué (public narrative).
- **Specificity wins:** quantities, timetables, named assets, chain of command — the staff executes what you write; ambiguity = failure.
- **Portfolio discipline:** never overstep scope (a Secretary of State ordering airstrikes gets rejected); track power usage; combine powers via JPDs; protect covert assets.
- **Crisis arc:** end goal + steps; small portfolios can win (Minister of Tourism archetype); prepare a rejuvenation storyboard for assassination/reassignment.
- **Tempo:** after every update, draft 1–2 directives in minutes; circulate with "add as you see fit and sign."
- **Joint crisis:** the other room is an actor — coordinate arcs, use communiqués, anticipate cross-room consequences.

## 14. COMMITTEE PLAYBOOKS (skill 46)

- **GA:** bloc math + drafting leadership; simple majority; resolution is the trophy.
- **UNSC:** binding language, P5 veto dynamics, charter basis (Ch. VI vs VII); win as the drafting bridge between veto blocs.
- **ECOSOC/commissions:** technical credibility, funding routes, SDG linkage, capacity-differentiated obligations.
- **Specialized agencies:** mandate boundaries — never ask a health agency to fix trade.
- **Historical:** date fence is absolute; period sources; butterfly-effect awareness.
- **Crisis:** directives + arcs, fastest pace; **Joint crisis:** cross-room coordination; **Legal/ICJ:** sources of law, not politics.

## 15. SOURCE ARSENAL (skill 47)

- **Symbols:** A/RES/ (GA), S/RES/ (SC), E/RES/ (ECOSOC); full text at `undocs.org/<symbol>`.
- **UN Digital Library** (digitallibrary.un.org): resolutions, votes, sponsors, drafts, meeting records.
- **Data by family:** WHO (health), UNHCR/IOM (refugees), World Bank/IMF (economy), SIPRI/IISS (arms), IPCC/UNFCCC (climate), OHCHR (human rights), UN Treaty Collection (treaties).
- **Country stance:** foreign ministry, permanent mission statements, explanations of vote.
- **Search ops:** `site:un.org`, `"explanation of vote" "Country X"`, `filetype:pdf`.
- **Traps:** NGO factoids ≠ official stats; GA resolutions recommend, SC can bind; verify date/scope always.
