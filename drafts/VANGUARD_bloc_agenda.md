## PART I — THE AGENDA (THE PROBLEM)

**One-line problem statement for lobbying and for the chair:**

> *How does the international community assess and address the transboundary ecological consequences of nuclear proliferation — the contamination that has already crossed borders, the risks that may cross them next, and the communities that were never asked to bear either?*

This is the exact language you use when the chair asks "what is your bloc's agenda?" — plain, within the committee's mandate, and broad enough to hold every clause without drifting off-topic.

### The Six Gaps (each one is a clause-bait)

**GAP 1 — THE MEASUREMENT GAP** — *What we cannot see*
> Transboundary radiological contamination from decades of nuclear testing and production spans borders, but there is no shared standard for measuring it — no common baseline, no harmonized data, no interoperable platform.
*Why it matters:* You cannot remediate what you have not measured. Without a common standard, one state's "safe" is another's "crisis."
*Committee hook:* CTBT International Monitoring System exists, IAEA monitoring exists, but they are not stitched into one ecological baseline.

**GAP 2 — THE WARNING GAP** — *What we cannot foresee*
> When a nuclear or radiological incident occurs, neighbouring States learn too slowly — there is no reliable transboundary early-warning or rapid-notification mechanism that covers all pathways (air, water, soil, food chain).
*Why it matters:* Fukushima's plume was detected globally in days; Zaporizhzhia runs on one power line. Time is the difference between "incident" and "catastrophe."

**GAP 3 — THE CAPACITY GAP** — *Who cannot measure*
> Developing States, coastal, archipelagic, landlocked-legacy-affected and SIDS lack the infrastructure, training and technology to monitor radiation or respond to emergencies — the burden falls on those least equipped to carry it.
*Why it matters:* The poison is democratic; the protection is not. This is the equity core that recruits the Global South.

**GAP 4 — THE CLEANUP GAP** — *What we do not fix*
> Legacy contamination — from test sites (Semipalatinsk, Moruroa, Lop Nor, Nevada, Maralinga, Pacific atolls), production sites (Hanford, Mayak) and waste stockpiles — remains unremediated, with no agreed framework for safe management, long-term monitoring, and environmental remediation.
*Why it matters:* The contamination is 80 years old. The framework is 0 days old.

**GAP 5 — THE ACCOUNTABILITY GAP** — *Who owes what*
> The consequences are transboundary but the responsibility is opaque — there is no mechanism that determines who contributes to monitoring, warning, and remediation, or how affected communities are supported.
*Why it matters:* Without a voluntary, consent-based burden-sharing model, the only alternative on offer will be a liability model — which France, Russia, UK, US and China will kill on sight, taking our paper with it.

**GAP 6 — THE PROTECTION GAP** — *What is still at risk*
> Nuclear facilities in conflict zones remain exposed, and a single incident could release contamination across borders that no assessment framework yet covers.
*Why it matters:* One shell at Zaporizhzhia matters more ecologically than any speech in this room. The IAEA's safety pillars already say so.

> **Lobby line (30 seconds, use this to win the agenda vote):**
> "Six gaps, one question: the poison has already crossed every border — when does the response? Every clause this bloc will write closes one of these gaps. No mandate overreach. No doctrine war. Just gaps this committee can actually close."

## PART IV — STRATEGIC DESIGN NOTES (INTERNAL — DO NOT CIRCULATE)

### Why this agenda is the best agenda for this bloc — and for Japan without them knowing

**S1 — ALPS Shield (Japan's biggest vulnerability, now your biggest armour):**
Every monitoring clause says "IAEA as technical reference point and benchmark, with monitoring data arising from IAEA-supported processes." That is the exact sentence that legitimizes Japan's ALPS discharge as IAEA-verified and far below WHO limits. China cannot attack Fukushima without attacking the clause she herself would have to sign. She invited the shield by signing the agenda.

**S2 — Scope Shield (Japan's second vulnerability):**
PP8 limits the resolution to ecological threats *arising from the development, testing, production and proliferation of nuclear weapons and their associated legacy sites*, while *noting* that civil operational releases are governed by existing IAEA safety standards. Translation: the agenda debates *weapons-ecology*, not civil energy. Japan's restarts (now ~30% of electricity mix toward 2040 target) stay off the floor.

**S3 — Fukushima-Flip (liability into credential):**
PP11 draws on "the world's only decade-long, large-scale post-accident environmental remediation experience" — nobody names Fukushima, everybody reads Fukushima. The only State that has actually *cleaned* a large-scale contamination becomes the teacher, not the accused. The agenda's REMEDIATE layer is Japan-shaped.

**S4 — Industry Lane (payoff for restarts):**
OP11 invites newcomer safeguards-by-design support from experienced States and the IAEA. France, Canada and Japan (all civilian nuclear operators) can sign it proudly. The agenda protects the peaceful-uses lane while appearing purely ecological.

**S5 — September Custody (the trapdoor):**
OP8 *welcomes* the 1 September International Meeting on Victim Assistance and Environmental Remediation and asks the SG to transmit its outcomes. Kazakhstan/Kiribati/Mexico authored that meeting. The bloc that welcomes it *owns* its follow-up. Whoever holds OP8 holds the agenda's future.

**S6 — Hub Candidacy (the quiet bid):**
OP1's Framework operates through existing institutions *and* requests a SG report to the 82nd session. The State that offers a data node / training hub (Japan's Rokkasho-adjacent labs, JAEA experience) inherits the Framework's next address. The agenda never says "hosted in Japan" — the *implementation report* will.

### Red Lines (if Mexico or any member pushes)

- **NO** "States *shall* / *must* / *will be subject to military action*" — replace with *encourages / invites / calls upon*.
- **NO** "strict state liability / assessed megatonnage / victim States bear zero burden" — replace with *voluntary, consent-based, existing-mechanism* language (the Anab´s Barbados/South Africa fixes already set the pattern).
