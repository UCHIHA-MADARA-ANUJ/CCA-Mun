### PREAMBULAR PARAGRAPHS

**PP1** *Guided by* the purposes and principles of the Charter of the United Nations, including the promotion of international cooperation in solving international problems of an economic, social and humanitarian character,

**PP2** *Recalling* that ecological degradation respects no border, and that radiological contamination, once released, is a shared inheritance of all States,

**PP3** *Recalling* its resolutions 78/240 of 2023, 79/60 of 2024 and 80/56 of 2025 on addressing the legacy of nuclear weapons, and *welcoming* the convening of the first International Meeting on Victim Assistance and Environmental Remediation on 1 September 2026, [KZ/MX anchor]

**PP4** *Noting* the thirtieth anniversary of the opening for signature of the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty on 24 September 2026 and the contribution of the International Monitoring System to environmental and radiological detection,

**PP5** *Reaffirming* the central role of the International Atomic Energy Agency in establishing nuclear safety and radiation-protection standards, and *recalling* the Convention on Early Notification of a Nuclear Accident (1986) and the Convention on Assistance in the Case of a Nuclear Accident or Radiological Emergency (1986),

**PP6** *Taking note with appreciation* of the establishment of the independent UN Scientific Panel on Effects of Nuclear War and anticipating its 2027 report, whose climatic, radiological and agricultural findings will inform this framework,

**PP7** *Recalling* existing international legal instruments relevant to nuclear weapons — including the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons and the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons — **for their respective States parties**, without prejudice to the sovereign positions of any State, [the formula — Rule 2 made flesh]

**PP8** *Emphasizing* that the present resolution addresses ecological threats arising from the development, testing, production and proliferation of nuclear weapons and their associated legacy sites, while *noting* that civil operational releases are governed by existing IAEA safety standards and verification mechanisms, [S2 scope shield — non-negotiable]

**PP9** *Mindful* of the humanitarian legacy of Hiroshima and Nagasaki on their 81st anniversaries, of Semipalatinsk, Moruroa, Lop Nor and all testing grounds, and of affected communities whose testimony anchors this work, [MX/KZ/SA]

**PP10** *Recognizing* the particular exposure of coastal, archipelagic, small-island and landlocked legacy-affected developing States, and their need for capacity, data access and technical cooperation, [PH/IN/PIF]

**PP11** *Recognizing also* the value of drawing upon the world's only decade-long, large-scale post-accident environmental remediation experience, which demonstrates that scientifically guided recovery is possible, [S3 Fukushima-flip — nobody names it, everybody reads it]

### OPERATIVE PARAGRAPHS

**OP1.** *Decides to establish* a **Global Framework for Nuclear-Ecological Monitoring, Response and Remediation** ("the Framework"), operating through existing institutions — the IAEA, UNEP, the CTBTO PrepCom and relevant regional bodies — without establishing new organs, and *requests* the Secretary-General to report on its activities to the General Assembly at its eighty-second session; **[JAPAN — UMBRELLA, NON-NEGOTIABLE]**

**OP2.** *Calls upon* Member States to work toward harmonized standards for environmental radiation-monitoring data and *invites* the IAEA to serve as the technical reference point for data quality and comparability, with monitoring data arising from IAEA-supported processes as the benchmark for international assessment; **[SWITZERLAND — L1 · S1 ALPS shield embedded: IAEA data = gold standard]**

**OP3.** *Encourages* the development of regional real-time monitoring networks and national node laboratories, and *invites* States with advanced monitoring systems to offer training, calibration services and equipment access to States building capacity; **[KOREA+JAPAN]**

**OP4.** *Invites* the IAEA, within existing mandates, to strengthen integration between national early-warning arrangements and its emergency mechanisms, and *encourages* Member States to conclude bilateral and regional protocols for transboundary notification, drill-sharing and mutual assistance, particularly benefiting coastal and archipelagic States; **[PHILIPPINES]**

**OP5.** *Calls upon* States in a position to do so to expand capacity-building, technical cooperation and infrastructure support for developing States in radiation detection, environmental sampling and emergency preparedness, including through South–South and triangular cooperation and existing international training centres; **[INDIA — GCNEP lane]**

**OP6.** *Encourages* the exchange of best practices on the safe, consent-based, long-term management of radioactive waste, including deep geological disposal, transparent siting processes, community consent models and owner-funded financing mechanisms; **[CANADA — live DGR receipts]**

**OP7.** *Invites* Member States with operating or decommissioning nuclear facilities to share environmental-monitoring experience from post-shutdown and decommissioning phases, and *requests* the Framework to compile a technical digest of decommissioning monitoring practices; **[GERMANY]**

**OP8.** *Welcomes* the International Meeting on Victim Assistance and Environmental Remediation of 1 September 2026, *calls upon* Member States to participate at high level and to share relevant national experience, *requests* the Secretary-General to transmit its outcomes to the General Assembly, and *invites* voluntary contributions toward future victim-assistance and remediation cooperation; **[MEXICO + KAZAKHSTAN — September custody, W1 in text]**

**OP9.** *Reaffirms* that attacks or threats of attack against safeguarded nuclear facilities risk the release of radiological material and constitute a matter of grave ecological and humanitarian concern, *calls upon* all States to respect applicable international law in this regard, and *invites* the IAEA Director General to continue reporting on environmental-monitoring implications at safeguarded sites affected by military activities; **[IRAN — caged lane: consensus-IAEA lineage (GC Res. 407/533 principle), names no state]**

**OP10.** *Commends* regional and bilateral verification and confidence arrangements, such as the Brazilian–Argentine Agency for Accounting and Control of Nuclear Materials, as models of sustained technical trust-building, and *encourages* the sharing of their operational experience with interested regions; **[BRAZIL]**

**OP11.** *Encourages* States pursuing new civil nuclear programmes to demonstrate the highest applicable safety, safeguards and environmental standards from the outset, and *invites* experienced States and the IAEA to support such newcomer safeguards-by-design approaches; **[UAE/EGYPT flex slot — S4 industry lane]**

**OP12.** *Decides* to remain seized of the matter through a standing agenda review at its eighty-third session, with a voluntary implementation report by the Secretary-General drawing on Framework partners. **[follow-through / feasibility]**
