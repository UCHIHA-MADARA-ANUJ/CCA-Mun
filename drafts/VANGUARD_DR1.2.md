## PREAMBULAR PARAGRAPHS

**PP1.** *Guided by* the purposes and principles of the Charter of the United Nations, including the promotion of international cooperation in solving international problems of an economic, social and humanitarian character,

**PP2.** *Recalling* that ecological degradation respects no border, and that radiological contamination, once released, is a shared inheritance of all States,

**PP3.** *Recalling* its resolutions 78/240 of 2023, 79/60 of 2024 and 80/56 of 2025 on addressing the legacy of nuclear weapons and providing victim assistance and environmental remediation to States affected by the use or testing of nuclear weapons, tabled by the Republic of Kazakhstan and the Republic of Kiribati, and the international meeting on victim assistance and environmental remediation convened pursuant thereto, [MX anchor + KAZ credit]

**PP4.** *Noting* the thirtieth anniversary of the opening for signature of the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty on 24 September 2026 and the contribution of the International Monitoring System to environmental and radiological detection,

**PP5.** *Reaffirming* the central role of the International Atomic Energy Agency in establishing nuclear safety and radiation-protection standards, and *recalling* the Convention on Early Notification of a Nuclear Accident (1986) and the Convention on Assistance in the Case of a Nuclear Accident or Radiological Emergency (1986),

**PP6.** *Taking note with appreciation* of the establishment of the independent UN Scientific Panel on Effects of Nuclear War and anticipating its 2027 report,

**PP7.** *Recalling* existing international legal instruments relevant to nuclear weapons — including the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons and the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons — for their respective States parties, without prejudice to the sovereign positions of any State, [the formula]

**PP8.** *Emphasizing* that the present resolution addresses ecological threats arising from the development, testing, production and proliferation of nuclear weapons and their associated legacy sites, while *noting* that civil operational releases are governed by existing IAEA safety standards and verification mechanisms, [S2 scope shield]

**PP9.** *Mindful* of the humanitarian legacy of Hiroshima and Nagasaki on their 81st anniversaries, of Semipalatinsk, Moruroa and all testing grounds, and of affected communities whose testimony anchors this work, [MX/SA]

**PP10.** *Recognizing* the particular exposure of coastal, archipelagic, small-island and landlocked legacy-affected developing States, and their need for capacity, data access and technical cooperation, [PH/IN/PIF]

**PP11.** *Recognizing also* the value of drawing upon the world's only decade-long, large-scale post-accident environmental remediation experience, which demonstrates that scientifically guided recovery is possible, [S3 Fukushima-flip]

**PP12.** *Reaffirming* the commitment of Member States to multilateral diplomacy, nuclear non-proliferation, arms control and the shared vision of a world free from nuclear weapons, [CANADA #1]

**PP13.** *Recalling* the resolutions of the General Conference of the International Atomic Energy Agency, including GC(XXIX)/RES/444 of 1985 and GC(XXXIV)/RES/533 of 1990, affirming that any armed attack on and threat against nuclear facilities devoted to peaceful purposes constitutes a violation of the principles of the Charter of the United Nations, international law and the Statute of the Agency, [IRAN — lineage anchor]

**PP14.** *Recognizing* the role of international scientific cooperation, including through the International Atomic Energy Agency, in strengthening the capacity of States to detect, assess and address radiological risks, and the contribution of such cooperation to transparency and confidence among States, [IRAN — science lane]

**PP15.** *Recognizing* the historic decision of the Republic of Kazakhstan to close the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site on 29 August 1991 and to renounce the world's fourth-largest nuclear arsenal, comprising approximately 1,410 nuclear warheads, as a landmark act of voluntary disarmament, [KAZAKHSTAN #1]

**PP16.** *Noting* the establishment and operation of the International Atomic Energy Agency Low-Enriched Uranium (LEU) Bank in Oskemen, Kazakhstan, as a secure, non-proliferation assurance-of-supply mechanism for eligible Member States, under the Agency's ownership, control and safeguards, [KAZAKHSTAN #2]

## OPERATIVE PARAGRAPHS

**OP1.** *Decides to establish* a Global Framework for Nuclear-Ecological Monitoring, Response and Remediation ("the Framework"), operating through existing institutions — the IAEA, UNEP, the CTBTO Preparatory Commission and relevant regional bodies — with respect for the sovereignty of all States, without establishing new organs or imposing new financial obligations on Member States, and *requests* the Secretary-General to report on its activities to the General Assembly at its eighty-second session; **[JAPAN #1 — UMBRELLA]**

**OP2.** *Calls upon* Member States to work toward harmonized standards and standardized scientific benchmarks for environmental radiation-monitoring data, including for transboundary topsoil and water radiation levels, and *invites* the IAEA to serve as the technical reference point for data quality and comparability, with monitoring data arising from IAEA-supported processes as the benchmark for international assessment; **[SWITZERLAND #1 — S1 ALPS shield + KAZAKHSTAN #4 reframed — standards]**

**OP3.** *Encourages* Member States, in cooperation with the IAEA and relevant international and regional organizations, to establish and strengthen regional environmental-radiation monitoring networks, including IAEA-coordinated monitoring arrangements, for the continuous assessment of radiation levels in transboundary air, water, soil, marine and agricultural systems, including mechanisms for timely data-sharing and early notification of affected States, with data-sharing protocols that respect national sovereignty and applicable international obligations; **[INDIA #1 + PHILIPPINES #1 + SWITZERLAND #1 + BARBADOS #1 + EGYPT #1 — merged]**

**OP3A.** *Urges* former testing States and possessor nations to share legacy geophysical, radiological and atmospheric testing data with affected host States on a voluntary, confidential basis, in order to facilitate the accurate mapping of radioactive hot spots and the assessment of long-term ecological risks; **[KAZAKHSTAN #3 — legacy data-sharing]**

**OP4.** *Encourages* Member States to designate national emergency contact points and to strengthen rapid-notification mechanisms for nuclear or radiological incidents with potential transboundary ecological consequences, building upon the Convention on Early Notification of a Nuclear Accident (1986); **[INDIA #2 + PHILIPPINES #1 + EGYPT #3 — merged]**

**OP5.** *Calls upon* the international community, particularly technologically and financially capable Member States and relevant UN agencies, to provide technical assistance, capacity-building and appropriate technology transfer to developing and vulnerable coastal States for radiation monitoring, environmental assessment, emergency preparedness and remediation of contaminated ecosystems, including through voluntary South–South and triangular cooperation programmes for the exchange of expertise, monitoring technologies, assessment methodologies and emergency-response training; **[INDIA #3 + PHILIPPINES #2 — merged]**

**OP6.** *Encourages* Member States to conduct environmental impact assessments for nuclear activities with potential transboundary consequences, and to consult potentially affected neighbouring States before undertaking activities that may pose significant ecological risks, in accordance with national circumstances and applicable international obligations; **[SWITZERLAND #3 — the EIA asset]**

**OP7.** *Calls upon* Member States to establish comprehensive radioactive waste management strategies, prioritizing deep geological disposal, consent-based community siting frameworks and owner-funded financing models, and *encourages* the exchange of best practices in this regard; **[CANADA #3 — waste lane]**

**OP7A.** *Encourages* enhanced cooperation with the International Atomic Energy Agency to strengthen environmental monitoring, radiation detection and nuclear waste verification mechanisms, building on the Agency's established technical cooperation and safety standards; **[CANADA #2 — IAEA monitoring cooperation]**

**OP8.** *Encourages* the establishment, through relevant UN bodies and the IAEA, of a voluntary mechanism for the independent assessment and public reporting of significant ecological risks arising from nuclear activities, including testing and proliferation-related contamination, while protecting legitimate national-security information; **[SOUTH AFRICA #1 reframed — UNEAVM]**

**OP9.** *Encourages* the establishment, through the UN Environment Programme, the International Atomic Energy Agency and the United Nations Office for Disarmament Affairs, of a voluntary International Radiological Damage and Ecological Remediation Fund to support affected States in ecological restoration, water decontamination, soil restoration and long-term environmental monitoring, including at legacy nuclear test sites, with contributions from States in a position to do so, including those whose nuclear activities have contributed to transboundary contamination, and without prejudging legal liability; **[MEXICO #1 reframed + SOUTH AFRICA #2 reframed + KAZAKHSTAN #1 reframed — merged fund]**

**OP9A.** *Encourages* voluntary cooperation with the IAEA for environmental monitoring along borders and at legacy nuclear test sites, ensuring that environmental soil sampling, groundwater testing and radiation monitoring occur exclusively with the explicit invitation and sovereign authorization of the host State, and *requests* the IAEA to report on such cooperation to the General Assembly; **[MEXICO #2 reframed + KAZAKHSTAN #2 reframed — verification]**

**OP9B.** *Recalls* the contribution of nuclear-weapon-free zones, including those established by the Treaties of Tlatelolco and Semipalatinsk, to regional security, environmental protection and the rehabilitation of territories contaminated by past nuclear testing, and *encourages* States parties to such zones to continue cooperation in this regard; **[MEXICO #3 reframed + KAZAKHSTAN — zones]**

**OP10.** *Encourages* Member States to strengthen transboundary notification and coordinated emergency-response arrangements for significant nuclear incidents or detected radioactive releases, building upon the 1986 Early Notification and Assistance Conventions, with the consent of affected States; **[SOUTH AFRICA #3 reframed — TNEEP]**

**OP11.** *Encourages* Member States, relevant UN agencies and international organizations to develop voluntary cooperative programmes for long-term assessment and restoration of ecosystems affected by radioactive contamination, including joint environmental assessments, biodiversity conservation, agricultural and marine ecosystem monitoring, and the strengthening of national preparedness and response capacities in affected and at-risk States, with particular attention to affected Indigenous peoples, downwind communities, vulnerable developing States and Small Island Developing States; **[BARBADOS #2 + #3 — merged]**

**OP12.** *Welcomes* the international meeting on victim assistance and environmental remediation, *calls upon* Member States to participate at high level, to share relevant national experience and to ensure that technical and humanitarian frameworks translate into practical, local-level soil decontamination and community-level remediation efforts, *requests* the Secretary-General to transmit its outcomes to the General Assembly, and *invites* voluntary contributions toward future victim-assistance and remediation cooperation; **[MEXICO + JAPAN #2 + KAZAKHSTAN #5 — September custody]**

**OP13.** *Reaffirms* that any armed attack on and threat against nuclear facilities devoted to peaceful purposes constitutes a violation of the principles of the Charter of the United Nations, international law and the Statute of the International Atomic Energy Agency, that armed attacks on nuclear facilities could result in radioactive releases with grave consequences within and beyond the boundaries of the State which has been attacked, *calls upon* all States to refrain from such attacks and threats of attack and to respect applicable international law in this regard, and *invites* the IAEA Director General to continue providing factual reporting on the nuclear safety, security and safeguards situation at safeguarded sites affected by military activities, including any radiological or environmental implications; **[IRAN — caged lane]**

**OP13A.** *Calls upon* all States to cooperate fully with the International Atomic Energy Agency in the exercise of its verification functions, *encourages* the restoration and strengthening of Agency access to nuclear material and facilities subject to safeguards, in accordance with the respective safeguards agreements of the States concerned, and *invites* the Agency to continue reporting to its Board of Governors and General Conference on the implementation of safeguards, including at sites affected by military activities; **[IRAN — access/verification lane]**

**OP14.** *Calls for* strengthened international technical cooperation for the long-term assessment and remediation of contaminated land, water and ecosystems, with particular attention to communities affected by transboundary nuclear contamination; **[EGYPT #2 — international fund + SWITZERLAND #2 — merged]**

**OP15.** *Encourages* Member States pursuing or operating civil nuclear programmes, in particular newcomer States, to establish and strengthen national radiation-monitoring institutions, regulatory frameworks and emergency-response capacities, and *invites* relevant international organizations and experienced States to support such efforts, including through regional cooperation among archipelagic and coastal States; **[PHILIPPINES #3 — PhilAtom/newcomer lane]**

**OP16.** *Reaffirms* the inalienable right of States to develop and use nuclear energy for peaceful purposes, in accordance with the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons and their respective safeguards agreements, and *encourages* international scientific and technical cooperation in this regard under IAEA supervision; **[IRAN — peaceful-uses/science lane]**

**OP17.** *Encourages* Member States, in cooperation with relevant international organizations and civil society, to support the documentation, preservation and transmission of the testimonies of survivors of the use and testing of nuclear weapons, including through education programmes, remembrance initiatives and survivor testimony archives, in recognition of the irreplaceable contribution of such testimony to the humanitarian and environmental record; **[JAPAN #3 — hibakusha testimony lane]**

**OP18.** *Decides* to remain seized of the matter through a standing agenda review at its eighty-third session, with a voluntary implementation report by the Secretary-General drawing on Framework partners. **[follow-through]**
