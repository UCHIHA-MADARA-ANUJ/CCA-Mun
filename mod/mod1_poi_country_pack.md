# MOD 1 — COUNTRY-BY-COUNTRY WRITTEN POI ARSENAL

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Final MOD 1 topic:** Assessing transboundary radiological contamination arising from the legacies of past nuclear testing and production

**Committee roster used:** 35 named portfolios, including Japan

**Coverage:** Five long-form written POIs for each of 34 other delegations

**Total:** 170 fact-screened MOD 1 POIs + Japan defence

**Format:** First sentence = complete trap; second sentence = demand for implementable text

**Status:** Floor-ready · 21 August 2026

---

## HOW TO USE THIS PACK FOR FULL MARKS

- Submit the **full written POI**. If asked to read it aloud, use only the first sentence.
- Select the POI matching the speaker’s actual argument; do not send five questions to one delegate.
- Use approximately two accountability traps, two swing-State bridge questions and two friendly technical lobs.
- A high-scoring POI contains one verified premise, one dilemma and one request for implementable policy.
- Never allege contamination where only a possible pathway is known. Ask for assessment or data.
- Never confuse testing/production legacies with reactor accidents or routine safeguarded civilian activity.
- Never expose VANGUARD coordination through a hostile question to a member.

### Priority targets

1. France — 210 tests and three consecutive NO votes.
2. Russia — Soviet archives, Kazakhstan and Arctic legacies.
3. United Kingdom — Australia/Pacific records and three NO votes.
4. United States — Marshall Islands, Lucky Dragon and production cleanup.
5. China — Lop Nur records and legacy-text abstentions.
6. DPRK — six tests without independent environmental accountability.
7. Kazakhstan — strongest friendly witness.
8. Germany — Wismut production-remediation expertise.
9. New Zealand — Pacific science and affected-community credibility.
10. South Africa — dismantled programme and production-accounting precedent.

## AUSTRIA — FRIENDLY TECHNICAL LOB

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “Vienna already hosts the IAEA and CTBTO system. Should this committee connect existing test-detection capacity to a permanent repository for historical fallout and remediation data? Japan asks the delegation to identify the operative clause it would support, including the responsible institution and reporting duty.”

**POI 2 — Source records / transparency:**

> “Should former testing States submit declassified plume, dose and site-monitoring records through an internationally accessible format rather than incompatible national archives? Please specify which records must be public and which genuinely security-sensitive information may remain protected, with reasons for each category.”

**POI 3 — Transboundary mechanism:**

> “Can CTBTO radionuclide expertise be used for environmental baseline work at former test sites without changing the CTBTO’s legal mandate? Japan requests an implementing authority, notification threshold, reporting interval and independent review process rather than a general assurance.”

**POI 4 — Victim assistance / remediation:**

> “Would Austria support applying victim-assistance and remediation principles even to States that have not joined the TPNW? Please identify who should fund the measure and how affected communities would participate in its design.”

**POI 5 — Resolution commitment:**

> “Should independent technical review compare national legacy reports so that affected States are not forced to accept a source State’s conclusions alone? Please state whether the delegation would vote for this language and, if not, provide a workable alternative before voting.”

**Fact lock:** Vienna hosts the IAEA and CTBTO Preparatory Commission; Austria hosts a CTBTO radionuclide laboratory at Seibersdorf.

**Strategic use:** Use Austria to prove Japan is building on existing institutions, not inventing a rival bureaucracy.

# JAPAN — EXPECTED MOD 1 POIS AND PERFECT ANSWERS

## “Is Fukushima not also transboundary contamination?”

> “This moderated caucus concerns past nuclear-testing and production legacies. Fukushima is a civilian accident and current regulated-release file assessed under separate IAEA safety processes. Japan supports shared monitoring in both contexts but will not collapse legally and technically distinct categories.”

## “Japan relies on the United States, which conducted the Marshall Islands tests.”

> “Alliance does not erase evidence. Japan has placed the verified Lucky Dragon record on this floor and supports the same disclosure, warning and remediation standard for allies and competitors.”

## “Japan has 44.4 tonnes of plutonium.”

> “That is separated civilian plutonium, publicly reported through annual declarations and subject to safeguards. It is not a past weapons-production or testing legacy. Japan distinguishes categories precisely so peaceful material is not misrepresented.”

## “Why should current governments pay for historical conduct?”

> “Japan’s framework does not prejudge legal liability. It asks States controlling records, expertise and resources to support monitoring and remediation while legal questions remain separate.”

## “Can Japan prove Lucky Dragon was outside the danger zone?”

> “Yes. Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum records that the vessel was well outside the declared US danger area and that all twenty-three crew were exposed to radioactive fallout.”

## “Does Japan claim every former test site remains equally dangerous?”

> “No. Contamination is heterogeneous. Japan asks for site-specific monitoring, comparable reporting and risk-based remediation—not blanket claims.”

# NEVER-USE MOD 1 POIS

- “Did Palomares involve a nuclear explosion?” — no; it was an accident involving conventional dispersal and is outside this MOD’s core testing/production scope.
- “Does Iran have nuclear weapons?” — unsupported.
- “Did fallout from every test cross an international border?” — not established; ask for pathways and data.
- “Is every part of the Kazakhstan test site unsafe?” — false; contamination is highly uneven.
- “Is Runit Dome cracking and leaking waste into the ocean?” — unsafe formulation; official assessments are more nuanced.
- “Did China officially conduct exactly [arsenal number]?” — do not mix warhead estimates with the verified number of tests.
- “Can the IAEA compel access to every former military site?” — no ordinary universal mandate exists.
- Any question that treats safeguarded civilian production as proven weapons production.

## FINAL SCORING DOCTRINE

The best MOD 1 POI does not merely accuse a testing State. It forces the delegation to choose among:

- releasing archives;
- accepting independent monitoring;
- funding technical capacity;
- including affected communities;
- reporting remediation outcomes; or
- placing a workable alternative into the resolution.

That is how Japan turns a question into evidence of leadership.
