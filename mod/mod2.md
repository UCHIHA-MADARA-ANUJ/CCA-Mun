# MOD 2 — EXISTING GAPS IN THE NUCLEAR-TREATY ARCHITECTURE

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Committee topic:** Deliberation on existing loopholes in nuclear treaties

**Speaking time:** 75 seconds

**Speech length:** 177 words — designed for approximately 69–71 seconds of speech plus deliberate pauses

**Status:** Final, fact-audited version · 21 August 2026

---

## SPEECH

[COLD]

The most dangerous loophole in nuclear law is not hidden inside a treaty. It is the space between them.

[CONTROLLED]

NPT Article III verifies non-diversion—not contamination or weapons-site remediation. Article IV protects peaceful use; it is not an environmental-liability code.

Article 3 of the Joint Convention normally excludes military spent fuel and waste unless declared—or transferred permanently to civilian management.

[FIRST PEAK]

**THE WASTE MOST PROTECTED BY SECRECY CAN ESCAPE INTERNATIONAL WASTE REVIEW.**

[ACCELERATE]

The CTBT has 179 ratifications—but no entry into force and no treaty on-site inspections. The TPNW contains victim-assistance and remediation duties—yet no nuclear-armed State is party.

One regime watches diversion. Another’s system detects tests. A third assists victims. Between them, military waste can escape international peer review.

Japan demands a connective layer: voluntary military environmental annexes, protected aggregates, and remediation reporting open to non-TPNW States.

[CLIPPED]

No designs. No operational locations. No fabricated mandate.

[CLIMB]

But no State should say: safeguarded, therefore safe; classified, therefore invisible; outside scope, therefore nobody’s responsibility.

[FINAL PEAK]

**COMPLIANCE WITH ONE TREATY IS NOT PERMISSION TO DISAPPEAR BETWEEN THE OTHERS.**

[QUIET]

I yield.

| Spoken claim | Status | Safe evidentiary basis |
|---|---|---|
| NPT Article III concerns safeguards/non-diversion | Verified | Article III requires safeguards for NNWS parties to verify fulfilment of non-proliferation obligations and prevent diversion from peaceful uses |
| Article IV protects peaceful nuclear use | Verified | Article IV recognizes the inalienable right to peaceful nuclear research, production and use in conformity with Articles I and II |
| NPT safeguards do not constitute an environmental-liability code | Verified legal distinction | Safeguards verify nuclear-material commitments; they do not create a universal weapons-site remediation or compensation regime |
| Joint Convention Article 3 normally excludes military/defence spent fuel and waste | Verified | Article 3(3) excludes military/defence programmes unless the State declares the material for Convention purposes; coverage applies after permanent transfer into exclusively civilian programmes |

| Treaty on-site inspections are unavailable before CTBT entry into force | Verified | CTBTO states that formal on-site inspection can occur only after entry into force |

## POINT-OF-ORDER FIREWALL

**If challenged that Article III safeguards do not themselves “verify”:**

> “Article III requires IAEA safeguards for the exclusive purpose of verifying fulfilment of non-proliferation obligations and preventing diversion. Japan’s shorthand accurately states that function.”

**If challenged that the Joint Convention can cover military waste:**

> “Correct—and Japan said ‘normally excludes’. Article 3 permits coverage when a State declares the material or after permanent transfer into exclusively civilian management. The exception proves that coverage is not automatic.”

**If told military waste is domestically regulated:**

> “Japan did not say it is universally unregulated. Japan said it can remain outside international peer review under the Joint Convention.”

**If challenged on CTBT inspections:**

> “The Preparatory Commission trains and prepares inspection capability. Formal treaty on-site inspections cannot be conducted until entry into force.”

**If a TPNW State says the Treaty already solves the problem:**

> “It creates strong obligations for its parties. Japan’s point is the participation gap: no nuclear-armed State is party, so a parallel voluntary route is still necessary.”

**If accused of manufacturing UNGA powers:**

> “Japan proposes voluntary annexes, protected aggregate reporting and remediation reporting. It neither compels inspection nor amends any treaty through this Assembly.”

**If attacked on Fukushima or Japanese plutonium:**

> “This intervention distinguishes legal categories. Safeguarded civilian material and regulated civilian operations are not military spent fuel or weapons-production waste. Environmental concerns remain assessable under their applicable safety frameworks.”
