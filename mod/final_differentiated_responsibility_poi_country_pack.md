# FINAL MOD — COUNTRY-BY-COUNTRY DIFFERENTIATED-RESPONSIBILITY POI ARSENAL

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Final MOD:** Determining differentiated responsibilities of nuclear-armed States, extended-deterrence beneficiaries, and non-nuclear disarmament advocates for monitoring, victim assistance, and remediation of transboundary radiological harm, without prejudging legal liability

**Coverage:** Five original, country-specific POIs for each of 34 other delegations

**Total:** 170 final-MOD POIs + Japan’s incoming-POI defence + rapid live-trigger questions

**Governing test:** **SOURCE — BENEFIT — CAPACITY**

**Status:** Final, fact-screened, named-target pack · 22 August 2026

---

## HOW TO USE THIS PACK

- Use the question matching the delegate’s actual claim; never fire all five at one speech.
- First identify the applicable nexus: **source of harm, formal security benefit, or capacity to act**.
- Source responsibility remains primary. Benefit and Capacity contributions are supplementary; they do not transfer another State’s liability.
- Give existing work full credit, then demand the next **measurable** contribution.
- Friendly POIs should let aligned States introduce machinery Japan needs. Hostile POIs should expose a missing contribution without inventing legal obligations.
- If a delegate says “we support cooperation,” force one choice: **what will you fund, host, register, release or deploy?**
- Do not describe the Source–Benefit–Capacity Matrix as established universal treaty law. It is Japan’s policy proposal.

### First-priority strikes

1. Norway — NATO protection plus verification/RANET capacity
2. Austria — humanitarian leadership versus measurable national implementation
3. Israel — emergency cooperation without an arsenal admission
4. Russia — source records, RANET capacity and Soviet legacies
5. United Kingdom — source responsibility plus information-barrier expertise
6. China — protected source reporting and remediation contribution
7. DPRK — minimum source-State environmental responsibility after six tests
8. United States — source/provider/capacity responsibility under one standard
9. Germany — nuclear-sharing benefit plus remediation capacity
10. Republic of Korea — explicit extended deterrence plus regional capacity

## AUSTRIA — ADVOCACY WITHOUT IMPLEMENTATION TEST

**POI 1 — Best strike:**

> “Beyond prohibition advocacy, what Austrian national resource—funding, technical expertise or training—will reach affected communities under the Capacity column?”

**POI 2 — Hosting trap:**

> “Does Austria consider hosting international institutions a national implementation contribution, or will it identify something Austria itself will fund, provide or deploy?”

**POI 3 — TPNW consistency:**

> “If TPNW parties ‘in a position to do so’ should assist, why should a capable humanitarian leader resist the same principle in a universal voluntary framework?”

**POI 4 — Existing-credit test:**

> “Which existing Austrian contribution should enter the responsibility register, and what measurable victim-assistance or remediation outcome does that contribution produce?”

**POI 5 — Source firewall:**

> “Can Austria accept a proportional Capacity contribution while preserving the primary financial and technical burden of States that created the harm?”

**Fact lock:** Austria is a TPNW party and leading humanitarian-disarmament advocate. Vienna hosts the IAEA and CTBTO Preparatory Commission, but Austria does not control those institutions.

**Strategic use:** Attack the gap between moral leadership and national implementation—not Austria’s genuine norm-building work.

# JAPAN — EXPECTED INCOMING POIS AND PERFECT ANSWERS

## “Is Japan not protected by US nuclear weapons?”

> “Yes. Japan stated that openly. Security benefit creates supplementary responsibility, and Japan accepts it through RANET training and exercises, monitoring capacity and international assistance. Source responsibility remains separate and primary.”

## “Does Japan benefit from the same US arsenal connected to Lucky Dragon?”

> “Yes—and alliance does not erase evidence. A United States test exposed all twenty-three Lucky Dragon crew, Japan applies source responsibility to allies and adversaries alike, and Japan separately accepts beneficiary responsibility.”

## “Is Japan assigning legal liability to umbrella States?”

> “No. Benefit responsibility is Japan’s proposed policy contribution, not liability for an ally’s conduct. The topic and Japan’s framework expressly avoid prejudging legal liability.”

## “Japan did not conduct the relevant tests, so why should it decide source duties?”

> “Japan is not adjudicating guilt. It proposes records, monitoring and assistance duties grounded in source nexus while affected States retain participation and every legal position remains reserved.”

## “Does Japan demand that Norway host nuclear weapons or disclose NATO plans?”

> “No. Japan makes no weapons-hosting claim. Norway’s role can consist entirely of RANET capability, laboratories, verification expertise, finance and warning while operations remain protected.”

## “Why attack Austria when it already leads disarmament?”

> “Japan credits Austria’s norm-building and existing contributions. The question is whether a capable advocate will also identify a measurable national resource reaching affected communities.”

## “Did Japan just call Israel a nuclear-armed State?”

> “No. Japan referred to Israel’s official strategic ambiguity and offered a Capacity-only route requiring no possession admission, arsenal count, location, readiness or doctrine.”

## “Why should advocates pay for damage they did not cause?”

> “They do not inherit source liability. Capable advocates provide proportional cooperation—expertise, training, translation, governance or finance—while source responsibility remains primary.”

## “Does this punish poor disarmament States?”

> “No. Capacity is proportional to fiscal and technical ability. A lower-capacity State may contribute community access or coordination while receiving laboratory and financial assistance.”

## “Is the Source–Benefit–Capacity Matrix established international law?”

> “No. It is Japan’s policy-allocation proposal. Existing instruments provide source and capacity precedents; Japan proposes the supplementary Benefit category.”

## “Does assistance admit liability?”

> “No. Warning, health support and remediation can proceed while attribution, compensation and legal responsibility remain separately determined.”

## “Can ordinary IAEA safeguards inspect military warheads under this plan?”

> “No. Japan creates no warhead-inspection power. Site access remains consent-based, and protected environmental cooperation excludes design, readiness and operational locations.”

## “Is Japan using RANET as if it deploys automatically?”

> “No. RANET assistance is request-based. Japan cites the network as existing capacity that States can register, exercise and offer when assistance is requested.”

## “Does Japan’s RANET centre erase all of Japan’s responsibility?”

> “No. It proves implementation, not complete discharge. Japan will enter current work in the register and remain accountable for further Benefit and Capacity contributions.”

## “What about Fukushima?”

> “Fukushima is a civilian accident governed by distinct safety processes, not a nuclear-weapons source legacy. Japan nevertheless accepts international monitoring and uses the same principles of calibrated data, warning and review.”

## “Japan has separated plutonium; is that not a weapons stockpile?”

> “No. Japan’s separated plutonium is declared civilian material under safeguards and annual public reporting. It must not be represented as an official weapons arsenal.”

## “Why should the source burden remain primary if everyone contributes?”

> “Because differentiation must not dilute causation. Wider contributions close capacity gaps; they do not purchase an exemption for the State connected to the original harm.”

## “Who decides whether a contribution is proportionate?”

> “Participating States declare capability, affected States identify needs and an auditable register compares resources, existing work and outcomes. Japan proposes review—not a supranational liability tribunal.”

## “Can one State fall into more than one category?”

> “Yes. The columns are cumulative nexuses, not exclusive labels. A State may be a source, beneficiary, capable contributor or affected recipient in different contexts.”

# NEVER-USE FINAL-MOD POIS

- “How many nuclear weapons does Israel officially possess?” — Israel has no official declared count.
- “Why is Norway hosting nuclear weapons?” — do not claim Norway hosts them.
- “Are umbrella beneficiaries legally liable for their protector’s tests?” — no universal rule establishes that.
- “Why does Austria contribute nothing?” — Austria contributes to institutions and disarmament; challenge measurable national implementation, not total absence.
- “Can the IAEA inspect every military warhead?” — ordinary safeguards do not provide that power.
- “Does RANET deploy automatically?” — no; assistance is request-based.
- “Why does Iran possess nuclear weapons?” — Iran is not an established nuclear-armed State.
- “Does TPNW bind nuclear-armed States?” — none is currently a party.
- “Did every nuclear test contaminate another country?” — not established.
- “Does assistance prove legal responsibility?” — no.
- “Should every advocate pay the same amount?” — contradicts differentiated capacity.
- “Can beneficiaries replace source-State finance?” — would dilute the matrix’s primary-source rule.
- Any POI describing SIPRI or FAS arsenal estimates as official national declarations.
