# MOD 3 — COUNTRY-BY-COUNTRY SOLUTION POI ARSENAL

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Solution MOD:** Developing a consent-based framework for coordinated monitoring, cross-border notification, and measurable remediation of weapons-related radiological threats

**Speaking time:** 60 seconds

**Coverage:** Five original, country-specific POIs for each of 34 other delegations

**Total:** 170 MOD 3 solution POIs + Japan’s incoming-POI defence

**Design rule:** No copied suffixes, no “Japan asks the delegation…”, no five questions with the same structure

**Status:** Final, fact-screened pack · 21 August 2026

---

## HOW TO USE THESE FOR MAXIMUM MARKS

- Pick the question matching what the delegate actually said. Do not send all five.
- The best solution POI forces an actor, trigger, data field, financing rule or outcome metric.
- Friendly POIs should help allies introduce a clause Japan needs.
- Hostile POIs should expose missing implementation, not merely accuse.
- If the answer is “international cooperation,” ask: **who acts, on what trigger, using which data?**
- Never ask a question whose factual premise you cannot defend from the fact lock.

### Priority order

1. China — consistency of evidence and independent review

# COUNTRY CARDS

## AUSTRIA — VIENNA-INSTITUTIONS TECHNICAL LOB

**POI 1 — Institutional integration:**

> “Vienna already hosts both the IAEA and CTBTO. Which exact function remains unperformed: environmental data standardization, cross-border notification, or remediation coordination—and which institution should perform it without exceeding its mandate?”

**POI 2 — Notification threshold:**

> “Would Austria trigger notification on a scientifically plausible transboundary pathway, or wait until measurable injury appears in another State? What prevents the second threshold from becoming warning after exposure?”

**POI 3 — TPNW bridge:**

> “Can the victim-assistance and remediation principles Austria defends under the TPNW be implemented through a parallel UNGA mechanism open to non-parties and nuclear-armed States?”

**POI 4 — Data access:**

> “Under what legal arrangement could CTBTO expertise support environmental assessment without pretending that IMS data are universally public or that CTBTO is a remediation authority?”

**POI 5 — Anti-duplication:**

> “If Austria opposes creating another institution, would it support a Secretary-General repository and technical roster that merely connect existing national, IAEA and CTBTO capabilities?”

**Fact lock:** Austria hosts the IAEA and CTBTO institutions in Vienna and strongly supports humanitarian nuclear policy.

**Strategic use:** Use Austria to validate institutional interoperability and kill the duplication objection.

# JAPAN — EXPECTED MOD 3 POIS AND PERFECT ANSWERS

## “Does this duplicate CTBTO?”

> “No. CTBTO detects nuclear explosions. Japan connects authorized detection outputs to environmental data standards, affected-State warning, requested assistance and measurable remediation.”

## “Can the IAEA inspect military sites under this framework?”

> “No. Site access remains consent-based and within existing mandates. Japan creates no compulsory inspection authority.”

## “Is RANET automatic?”

> “No. Assistance is requested and accepted by States. Japan proposes expanded preparedness, registered capabilities and regional exercises—not forced deployment.”

## “Does notification establish liability?”

> “No. Notification protects people while facts develop. Attribution, compensation and legal responsibility remain separate.”

## “Why should any detection trigger notification?”

> “It should not. Japan’s threshold requires credible evidence of a plausible transboundary pathway with potential radiological safety significance.”

## “Why build another fund or body?”

> “Japan proposes a voluntary assistance window and repository connecting existing systems—not a new regulator, inspection agency or assessed-contribution bureaucracy.”

## “What gives Japan credibility?”

> “Japan hosts an IAEA RANET Capacity Building Centre, supports international exercises and applies international monitoring in its own radiological files.”

## “Is this a disguised Fukushima shield?”

> “The scope is weapons-related radiological threats under this agenda. The standards remain technically consistent: calibrated data, uncertainty, international communication and independent review.”

# NEVER-USE MOD 3 POIS

- “Why does CTBTO not clean contaminated sites?” — remediation is not its mandate.
- “Why does RANET not deploy automatically?” — it is request-based by design.
- “Can UNSCEAR declare an emergency?” — no.
- “Can the IAEA compel military-site access?” — no.
- “Are all 337 IMS facilities operating?” — no; roughly 90% are operational.
- “Does notification prove liability?” — no.
- “Should any detected radionuclide trigger warning?” — scientifically unworkable.
- “Does remediation remove all radioactivity?” — no; it is justified, optimized and site-specific.
