# SUBSTANTIVE CHIT 04 — THE RESPONSIBILITY MATRIX

**Delegation:** Japan

**Committee:** United Nations General Assembly

**Conference:** CCA MUN ’26

**Agenda:** Assessing the ecological threats of nuclear proliferation on global boundaries

**MOD focus:** Determining differentiated responsibilities of nuclear-armed States, extended-deterrence beneficiaries, and non-nuclear disarmament advocates for monitoring, victim assistance, and remediation of transboundary radiological harm, without prejudging legal liability

**Submission mode:** Polished PDF substantive chit

---

## Exact submission

Every State wants authority in this debate. Every State must carry responsibility.

Japan proposes the **Responsibility Matrix**: responsibility follows the source of harm, the security benefit received, and the capacity to act. Source responsibility stays primary; every capable voice acquires an implementation burden.

### COLUMN ONE — SOURCE

States directly connected to nuclear testing or weapons production:

- preserve and index historical records of tests, production and releases;
- release non-sensitive plume, dose and environmental data;
- provide technical experts and site characterization;
- lead remediation, monitoring and measurable environmental outcomes.

**Source responsibility stays primary. It is not diluted by the contributions of others.**

### COLUMN TWO — BENEFIT

States whose security is protected by extended nuclear deterrence:

- provide early-warning nodes and emergency contacts;
- register assistance capacities under the IAEA Response and Assistance Network;
- contribute laboratories, verification research and exercises;
- support voluntary financing and risk-reduction diplomacy.

**This is supplementary policy responsibility — not liability for another State’s conduct.**

### COLUMN THREE — CAPACITY

Capable non-nuclear advocates:

- contribute laboratories, experts and training;
- support victim assistance and community participation;
- host repositories, workshops and translation services;
- provide independent review and audit.

**Contributions are proportional to fiscal and technical capacity; no State is assigned an equal financial quota.**

### JAPAN DECLARES ITS OWN COLUMNS

Japan is a beneficiary of United States extended deterrence; the United States has reaffirmed its commitment to the defence of Japan using the full range of its capabilities, including nuclear. Japan does not exempt itself. Security benefit creates responsibility.

Japan hosts an IAEA RANET Capacity-Building Centre in Fukushima Prefecture that has delivered international emergency-preparedness training since 2013; maintains monitoring and laboratory capacity; publishes nuclear-material data; and supports international assistance and verification research.

**Japan asks every beneficiary and every advocate: which column will you fill?**

### VOLUNTARY RESPONSIBILITY REGISTER

- contributions are recorded, not coerced;
- existing contributions receive credit;
- no double counting of the same contribution;
- outcomes are measured: victims assisted, samples analysed, land restored.

**A nuclear umbrella cannot deliver security above the border while ecological responsibility disappears below it.**

### DRAFT-READY RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Requests** the Secretary-General to establish, through existing institutions and without creating new organs, a voluntary Responsibility Register recording contributions to monitoring, victim assistance and remediation of transboundary radiological harm;
2. **Invites** source States to preserve and release non-sensitive environmental records and to lead remediation, with measurable outcomes reported to the Register;
3. **Encourages** extended-deterrence beneficiaries to contribute warning infrastructure, laboratories, training and voluntary financing, without creating liability for any other State’s conduct;
4. **Encourages** capable States to contribute expertise, victim assistance, independent review and community participation, proportional to capacity;
5. **Calls upon** all contributors to credit existing work and to avoid double counting;
6. **Decides** to remain seized of the matter.

### LEGAL FIREWALL

This matrix is a policy proposal, not an assertion of treaty law. It creates no automatic liability, assigns no equal burden between source and beneficiary, and neither assistance nor monitoring adjudicates compensation. Nothing in this matrix requires the disclosure of weapon designs, current operational locations or readiness information.

## Supporting architecture

### Why the matrix fits the debate

The committee has asked how responsibility should be differentiated across three categories of States. The Matrix answers with one test applied to all three: **source of harm, security benefit received, capacity to act.** It converts every delegation’s declared identity — source, beneficiary or advocate — into an implementation obligation, while leaving legal liability untouched.

### Existing contributions the Register credits

- Japan: IAEA RANET Capacity-Building Centre, Fukushima Prefecture; international emergency-preparedness training since 2013; published plutonium and nuclear-material data.
- Kazakhstan: hosting international attention on the Semipalatinsk legacy — 456 tests between 1949 and 1989 — and cooperation with the IAEA on site remediation.
- The United States: an official stockpile-transparency record and a Department of Energy cleanup programme whose remaining liability has been estimated above half a trillion dollars.
- The United Kingdom: published aggregate warhead ceiling and bilateral verification research under the UK–Norway Initiative.
- Norway: verification research protecting classified attributes through information barriers.
- South Africa: dismantlement of its nuclear-weapons programme and accession to the NPT.
- Austria: leadership in humanitarian-impact advocacy and host of IAEA and CTBTO institutions.
- Mexico, the Philippines and affected-State leaders: sustained advocacy for victim assistance and environmental remediation under General Assembly resolutions 78/240 and 79/60.

The Register records these contributions once, credits them, and measures outcomes — it does not score politics.

### Debate counters

**If a beneficiary says: “We are not a nuclear-weapon State; why should we contribute?”**
> NATO itself states that nuclear sharing ensures the benefits, responsibilities and risks of nuclear deterrence are shared among Allies. Japan accepts the same logic for its own security. The question is not whether beneficiaries contribute — it is which column they fill.

**If an advocate says: “We have no nuclear legacy; we owe nothing.”**
> Capacity is a column, not a fine. Laboratories, experts, victim assistance and independent review are proportional to capacity. No State is assigned an equal financial quota; every capable voice acquires an implementation burden.

**If a source State says: “We already do enough.”**
> Then the Register costs nothing: record it, credit it, and show the outcome. The Matrix does not demand more than a State can prove it is already doing.

### Precision declarations

- The NATO quotation is used as the Alliance’s own stated policy; it does not prove a legal duty of any member State.
- The Japan–U.S. dialogue statement is quoted as a diplomatic commitment, not as a treaty obligation.
- The RANET Capacity-Building Centre claim is limited to what the IAEA reports: training and workshops hosted since 2013.
- Resolution 79/60 requested the Secretary-General to convene a meeting in 2026; this chit does not claim the meeting has already occurred.
- TPNW Articles 6 and 7 bind States parties only; the chit cites them as design precedent, not as universal law.
- The DOE cleanup figure is an estimate of remaining liability reported by the GAO, not an official appropriation.
- The Responsibility Register is proposed as voluntary; nothing in the chit creates legal liability, equal quotas or compensation awards.
