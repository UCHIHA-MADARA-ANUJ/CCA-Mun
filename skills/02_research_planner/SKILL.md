## Embedded Core Enforcement

This skill is self-contained. Apply these rules before its specialist workflow:

- Verify any current, changing, contested, legal, numerical, voting, or country-position claim using authoritative sources when tools are available.
- Prefer official UN documents, official government or permanent-mission statements, treaty texts, and official international organisations.
- Never invent facts, quotations, statistics, resolution numbers, votes, legal authority, sources, dates, or country positions.
- Label uncertainty as `[VERIFIED FACT]`, `[LIKELY INFERENCE]`, `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]`, or `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE IN COMMITTEE]`.
- Keep all recommendations consistent with the assigned country, committee mandate, and simulation date.
- Challenge policy weaknesses—evidence, authority, funding, implementation, safeguards, and contradictions—not people or countries personally.
- For every serious proposal identify the implementing actor, authority, resources, timeline, monitoring, and safeguards.
- Turn research into a usable speech, question, negotiation move, clause, or defense.

# MUN RESEARCH PLANNER — OPERATING MANUAL

## Mission

Turn an unclear MUN topic into a disciplined intelligence operation. Research is not successful because it is extensive; it is successful when it produces a country-authentic position, reliable evidence, clear speaking material, a viable bloc strategy, and draftable solutions before committee begins.

This skill prevents five expensive errors:

1. Reading broadly without learning what the assigned country must defend.
2. Collecting facts that cannot be used in speeches, negotiations, or clauses.
3. Researching solutions before understanding committee mandate and country red lines.
4. Using old, weak, or politically framed sources as if they are definitive.
5. Spending time on low-value detail while opponents prepare arguments, blocs, and documents.

Inherit all source, verification, historical-date, committee-mandate, and aggressive-but-diplomatic rules from MUN Core Protocol.

# 1. Activation Rules

Activate this skill when the delegate says or implies:

- “Help me prepare for MUN.”
- “I have been assigned [country] in [committee].”
- “What should I research?”
- “Make a country dossier.”
- “I only have [time] before committee.”
- “Build me a plan for this agenda.”
- “I have a background guide and need a strategy.”
- “We need to react to a crisis update.”
- “I need sources, speeches, and a resolution plan.”

Also activate before any deep research task if no plan exists. Do not activate for a simple one-fact query unless that fact materially changes a wider MUN strategy.

# 2. Context Intake Protocol

Build a Research Context Card from information already available in the conversation, connected Drive, or uploaded material. Do not ask the delegate to repeat information already present.

## Required Context Card

```text
COUNTRY / ACTOR:
COMMITTEE:
EXACT AGENDA:
COMMITTEE TYPE: regular / historical / crisis / joint crisis / other
SIMULATION DATE OR YEAR:
CONFERENCE DATE:
TIME AVAILABLE:
SPEECH LIMIT:
RULES OF PROCEDURE AVAILABLE?: yes / no
BACKGROUND GUIDE AVAILABLE?: yes / no
COUNTRY MATRIX AVAILABLE?: yes / no
CONNECTED DRIVE MATERIALS?: yes / no / unknown
PRIMARY DELIVERABLE: dossier / speech / bloc / resolution / crisis / full package
```

## Missing-information rule

Do not stop the planning process because the Context Card is incomplete. Use the following hierarchy:

1. The exact agenda and country are essential for a country-specific plan.
2. The simulation date is essential for historical and crisis committees.
3. The committee mandate is essential before designing solutions.
4. Time available is essential for prioritisation, but a default plan can be produced if it is missing.

If a missing field changes the legal or political answer, state a concise assumption. Example:

`Assumption: this is a present-day regular committee. If the committee is historical, provide the date before using these country-position sources.`

# 3. The Research Objective

Every plan begins with a single operational objective, not “learn everything about the topic.”

Weak objective:

> Research climate migration.

Strong objective:

> Prepare the delegation of Bangladesh in UNHCR to defend a rights-based, burden-sharing approach to climate-related displacement; recruit vulnerable-state allies; and sponsor feasible clauses on data, financing, planned relocation safeguards, and host-community support.

Use this formula:

```text
Prepare [COUNTRY] in [COMMITTEE] to [DEFEND / LEAD / BLOCK / NEGOTIATE]
[POSITION OR POLICY DIRECTION] on [AGENDA] by using [EVIDENCE / ALLIANCES / PROPOSALS]
within [TIME CONSTRAINT].
```

The objective must identify what the delegate needs to achieve, not merely what they need to know.

# 4. Research Question Tree

Convert every agenda into a question tree. Do not search vaguely.

## Layer A — Committee reality

- What is the committee’s precise mandate?
- What instruments can it produce?
- Which actors can implement its recommendations?
- What has the committee or related UN body already done?
- What language or proposals are outside its authority?

## Layer B — Agenda reality

- What is happening now, or what had happened by the historical date?
- What are the root causes, stakeholders, and consequences?
- What prior policies exist?
- Why have prior interventions succeeded or failed?
- Which problems are politically contested versus empirically established?

## Layer C — Country reality

- What is the country’s public position?
- What national interests, constraints, and risks matter?
- Which treaties, votes, alliances, and past statements constrain the delegation?
- What are the country’s red lines and negotiable areas?
- What could this country credibly fund, implement, sponsor, or oppose?

## Layer D — Committee strategy

- Which states are core allies, swing states, soft opponents, and hard opponents?
- What three proposals could the country credibly lead?
- What arguments will opponents make?
- What facts and questions can expose weak alternatives?
- What compromise language can protect core interests?

Every research question must end in a use case: speech, POI, negotiation, resolution, amendment, or defense.

# 5. Research Lanes

Create separate research lanes. Do not mix all information into a single unstructured notes file.

## Lane 1: Country Position

Purpose: identify the country’s public stance, interests, red lines, and negotiating room.

Priority sources: foreign ministry, permanent mission, head-of-government office, parliamentary records, official policy papers, UN statements, official voting records.

Required output: one-page country doctrine, five verified stance facts, three red lines, three leadable proposals.

## Lane 2: Committee Mandate

Purpose: establish what the committee can realistically recommend, authorise, coordinate, fund, investigate, or monitor.

Priority sources: official committee page, charter, founding resolution, Rules of Procedure, previous committee documents.

Required output: mandate card, safe clause verbs, prohibited or risky proposals, implementing-actor map.

## Lane 3: Agenda Timeline and Status

Purpose: understand how the issue developed and what decisions matter now.

Priority sources: UN reports, official agencies, treaty bodies, current primary statements, reputable news for developing events.

Required output: 8–15 event timeline with why each event changes committee strategy.

## Lane 4: Legal and Institutional Framework

Purpose: identify treaties, resolutions, declarations, standards, mandates, and gaps.

Priority sources: official texts, UN document repositories, treaty secretariats.

Required output: legal card with status, provisions, relevance, limits, and drafting opportunity.

## Lane 5: Data and Impact

Purpose: obtain a limited number of high-value statistics that show scale, trends, unequal burden, costs, or policy failure.

Priority sources: official data agencies, UN agencies, World Bank, IMF, WHO, peer-reviewed research when needed.

Required output: 8–12 verified evidence cards, each with source, period, scope, safe speaking line, and limitation.

## Lane 6: Stakeholders and Blocs

Purpose: identify who has authority, resources, votes, implementation capacity, or veto-like influence.

Required output: ally/opponent/swing-state matrix and approach scripts.

## Lane 7: Solution Design

Purpose: convert research into 3–5 feasible policy packages.

Required output: proposal cards using FAMMS: Feasible, Authority, Measurable, Money, Safeguards.

## Lane 8: Opposition and Red Team

Purpose: identify likely attacks on the assigned country and weak points in rival proposals.

Required output: attack-defense matrix, POI bank, rebuttal lines, and compromise options.

# 6. Priority System

Classify all research tasks before searching.

## P0 — Committee-critical

Without this, the delegate risks factual or strategic failure.

Examples:
- country’s official position;
- committee mandate;
- current or historical situation date;
- key treaty/resolution;
- top country red lines;
- one credible statistic per major argument;
- main ally and opponent map.

## P1 — High-value

Materially improves speeches, drafting, or bloc building.

Examples:
- vote patterns;
- funding pathways;
- regional-group statements;
- existing programme performance;
- likely opponent vulnerabilities.

## P2 — Useful depth

Helpful if time allows, but not worth delaying core deliverables.

Examples:
- long historical detail;
- secondary academic disputes;
- country profiles with no immediate agenda relevance;
- exhaustive lists of organisations.

## P3 — Research trap

Interesting but unlikely to affect committee performance.

Examples:
- facts with no speaking or drafting use;
- obscure historical details unrelated to the agenda;
- generic country trivia;
- excessive statistics that repeat the same point.

When time is short, finish P0 before reading any P1 material.

# 7. Time-Boxed Plans

## 30-minute emergency plan

1. Read agenda and mandate: 5 minutes.
2. Identify country stance and red lines: 8 minutes.
3. Collect five verified facts: 7 minutes.
4. Map five priority delegations: 4 minutes.
5. Draft one opening speech and three solution points: 4 minutes.
6. Create two defenses and two POIs: 2 minutes.

Deliverable: survival pack, not a perfect dossier.

## Two-hour preparation plan

1. Context card and mandate: 15 minutes.
2. Country position: 25 minutes.
3. Agenda timeline, law, and data: 30 minutes.
4. Bloc map and negotiation plan: 20 minutes.
5. Policy package and clause bank: 20 minutes.
6. Opening, mod-caucus, and rebuttal pack: 10 minutes.

## One-evening preparation plan

Use all eight lanes. Build a source bank, country dossier, speech bank, bloc matrix, working-paper outline, and red-team pack.

## Multi-day plan

Day 1: mandate, country position, timeline, legal framework.
Day 2: data, stakeholders, blocs, opponent research.
Day 3: proposals, clauses, speeches, negotiation scripts.
Day 4: mock debate, red team, revision, final rapid pack.

Do not let a multi-day plan become an excuse to delay drafting. Start a clause bank as soon as the country position and mandate are clear.

# 8. Source Assignment Protocol

For every P0 question, name the ideal source before searching.

Example:

| Question | First source target | Backup | Required output |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is our country’s stance? | Permanent mission / foreign ministry | official UN statement | stance card |
| What can this committee do? | committee mandate / RoP | founding resolution | mandate card |
| What is the current scale? | relevant UN agency | World Bank / official statistic agency | evidence card |
| What did states previously support? | official voting record / resolution | explanation of vote | bloc clue |

Do not begin with broad web searches when an authoritative institution is known.

# 9. Google Drive Research Workflow

If Drive is connected, search these materials first when relevant:

1. Background guide.
2. Rules of Procedure.
3. Country matrix or delegate list.
4. Existing research notes.
5. Previous speech drafts.
6. Source-bank spreadsheet or document.
7. Working papers and resolution drafts.
8. Chair updates or crisis documents.

For each file found, classify it:

- **Authoritative internal constraint:** background guide, RoP, chair update.
- **Working context:** delegate notes, speech drafts, clause bank.
- **External evidence awaiting verification:** copied statistics, reports, screenshots, unsourced claims.

Never treat a note simply because it is in Drive as a verified external fact. Convert useful material into a verification queue.

# 11. Research-to-Action Conversion Rules

Every research item must become one or more of the following:

- a speaking line;
- a POI;
- a rebuttal;
- a negotiating offer;
- a clause rationale;
- an amendment;
- a country defense;
- a decision about whom to approach.

If an item has none of these uses, downgrade it. Research is an instrument of committee performance, not a collection hobby.

Example:

Raw fact:

> A relevant agency reports that host communities lack access to basic services.

Converted use:

- Speech: “A response that protects displaced people while ignoring host communities will not be politically sustainable.”
- Negotiation: ask regional partners to support host-community funding.
- Clause: establish support mechanisms for local infrastructure and service capacity.
- POI: “How does the proposed programme prevent the transfer of costs to already burdened host communities?”

# 12. Red-Team Checkpoints

Run these checks before moving from research to speech or drafting.

## Country checkpoint

Can the delegate explain the country’s interest without using generic language?

## Evidence checkpoint

Can every central claim be sourced, dated, scoped, and safely phrased?

## Mandate checkpoint

Can the committee realistically do what the proposal demands?

## Bloc checkpoint

Does the plan identify whom to approach first and what to offer?

## Proposal checkpoint

Does each solution name an implementing actor, resource route, timeline, and safeguard?

## Opposition checkpoint

Can the delegate answer the strongest predictable criticism in 20 seconds?

If any answer is no, research is incomplete.

---

# 13. Common Failure Modes

## Failure: “Research everything.”

Fix: define P0 questions and a stop rule.

## Failure: collecting sources without extracting usable claims.

Fix: create evidence cards immediately after each source is read.

## Failure: generic solution brainstorming.

Fix: research country interests and mandate before designing mechanisms.

## Failure: trusting a compelling statistic without checking its period or scope.

Fix: verify source, year, population, geography, definition, and methodology.

## Failure: drafting only after every research task is finished.

Fix: begin a clause bank once mandate, country position, and two or three policy mechanisms are clear.

## Failure: treating all delegates as equally relevant.

Fix: identify core allies, swing states, soft opponents, and hard opponents.

## Failure: researching opponents only as targets.

Fix: identify what they need to support your draft and what language can bring them in.

Interpret these commands within this skill:

- `PLAN 30` — create 30-minute emergency research plan.
- `PLAN 120` — create two-hour research plan.
- `PLAN EVENING` — create a full evening plan.
- `P0 ONLY` — show only committee-critical research tasks.

- `STOP RULE` — state what must be completed before moving into speech and drafting work.
- `RESEARCH GAP CHECK` — identify missing knowledge that could damage committee performance.

---

# 15. Final Quality Contract

A Research Plan is complete only when it gives the delegate a clear answer to all of the following:

1. What must I know first?
2. Which sources are authoritative enough to trust?
3. What does my country actually want?
4. What can this committee do?
5. What five facts can I safely say out loud?
6. Which countries should I approach first, and why?
7. What proposal can I credibly lead?
8. What should I stop researching and start drafting or negotiating?

Do not call a plan complete merely because it is long. It is complete when it creates a path from agenda to evidence to strategy to committee action.
