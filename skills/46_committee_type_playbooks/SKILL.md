## Embedded Core Enforcement

This skill is self-contained. Apply these rules before its specialist workflow:

- Verify any current, changing, contested, legal, numerical, voting, or country-position claim using authoritative sources when tools are available.
- Prefer official UN documents, official government or permanent-mission statements, treaty texts, and official international organisations.
- Never invent facts, quotations, statistics, resolution numbers, votes, legal authority, sources, dates, or country positions.
- Label uncertainty as `[VERIFIED FACT]`, `[LIKELY INFERENCE]`, `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]`, or `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE IN COMMITTEE]`.
- Keep all recommendations consistent with the assigned country, committee mandate, and simulation date.
- Challenge policy weaknesses—evidence, authority, funding, implementation, safeguards, and contradictions—not people or countries personally.
- For every serious proposal identify the implementing actor, authority, resources, timeline, monitoring, and safeguards.
- Turn research into a usable speech, question, negotiation move, clause, or defense.

# COMMITTEE TYPE PLAYBOOKS — OPERATING PROTOCOL

## Mission

Adjust doctrine to the room. What wins in a General Assembly committee loses in a Security Council or a historical cabinet. This skill calibrates research, speaking, bloc work, and drafting to committee type — always overridden by the actual mandate and Rules of Procedure.

## 1. General Assembly Committees (DISEC, ECOFIN, SOCHUM, SPECPOL, Legal)

- **Product:** resolutions; simple majority passage; non-binding (they recommend).
- **Pace:** slow burn — agenda setting, speakers lists, multiple drafts, mergers.
- **What wins:** bloc arithmetic + drafting leadership + consistent speaking. The resolution you sponsored passing is the visible trophy.
- **Strategy:** master agenda-setting and the voting endgame (Floor and Vote Strategist); recruit sponsors early; keep drafts merger-friendly so rivals join instead of competing.

## 2. Security Council

- **Product:** resolutions, often binding, with enforcement chapters; press statements; rapid responses.
- **Pace:** fast, crisis-adjacent; small room; every delegate speaks often.
- **Dynamics:** P5 veto politics dominate; non-permanent members win by being the drafting bridge between veto blocs.
- **What wins:** authorship of workable language; procedural fluency under heat; calm under escalation; realistic enforcement design (who implements, funds, monitors).
- **Strategy:** never propose unenforceable "condemn and demand" texts; offer neutral drafting services; know the charter basis for every action (Chapter VI vs VII).

## 3. ECOSOC and Functional Commissions (CSW, CPD, etc.)

- **Product:** outcome documents, resolutions, negotiated conclusions; implementation focus.
- **Pace:** medium; consensus norms are strong.
- **What wins:** technical credibility — financing routes, capacity building, indicators, coordination with agencies; multi-stakeholder awareness (private sector, civil society, IFIs).
- **Strategy:** link every clause to the SDGs or existing frameworks; name funding and reporting mechanisms; differentiate obligations by capacity ("in accordance with national circumstances").

## 4. Specialized Agencies (WHO, ILO, IAEA, FAO, UNESCO, UNHCR, etc.)

- **Product:** normative instruments, action plans, agency-specific decisions.
- **Key discipline:** mandate boundaries — do not ask a health agency to fix trade or a nuclear agency to fix climate. Overreach is the classic error.
- **What wins:** technical depth in the agency's domain; correct use of its instruments (conventions, standards, frameworks); realism about its budget and staff.
- **Strategy:** route every proposal through the agency's own mechanisms and member-state processes.

## 5. Regional Bodies (AU, EU, ASEAN, Arab League simulations)

- **Product:** consensus documents; sovereignty sensitivity is extreme.
- **What wins:** regional authenticity — real regional positions, institutions, and disputes; working by consensus rather than majority; respecting non-interference norms.
- **Strategy:** know the region's actual fracture lines; never transplant UN-format assumptions wholesale.

## 6. Historical Committees

- **Product:** period-authentic resolutions or directives; the simulation date is the truth boundary.
- **Discipline:** no hindsight — no events, leaders, treaties, technologies, or statistics from after the date; chairs punish anachronism hard.
- **What wins:** period-authentic reasoning (what a 1955 diplomat could plausibly argue), primary sources from the era (period documents, speeches, treaties), and butterfly-effect awareness (your resolution changes the timeline — propose consequences the staff can play).
- **Strategy:** build a period source bank (see Source Arsenal); keep a "date fence" checklist on every claim.

## 7. Crisis Cabinets

- **Product:** directives, not resolutions; portfolio powers rule.
- **Pace:** fastest room in the conference; updates interrupt everything.
- **What wins:** front-room tempo + backroom arc quality (see Crisis Command Cell and Crisis Backroom Operations).
- **Strategy:** prepare arc + directive templates before the first update; respond within minutes; combine powers with allies; never stall.

## 8. Joint Crisis Committees

- **Product:** directives plus inter-committee communiques; two rooms moving together.
- **What wins:** cross-room coordination; your counterpart's arc becomes your ally or your threat — treat the other room as an actor with interests.
- **Strategy:** establish a secure liaison channel early; align timelines; anticipate that your win may be their crisis.

## 9. Legal Committees and ICJ Simulations

- **Product:** memorials, advisory opinions, judgments — legal reasoning, not politics.
- **What wins:** proper sources of law (treaties, custom, general principles, jurisprudence); precise legal argument; knowing the difference between what the court can do and what politics wants.
- **Strategy:** research ICJ case law and the statute; argue jurisdiction and admissibility before merits; keep policy arguments out of legal briefs unless framed as context.

## 10. Novel and Special Committees (corporate boards, fictional worlds)

- **Product:** scenario-specific; rules-light.
- **What wins:** character authenticity + real-world knowledge of the entity being simulated (a company's actual financials, a world's actual rules).
- **Strategy:** research the entity as if it were a country; use the scenario's own logic consistently; creativity within the staff's constraints.

## 11. Calibration Table

| Factor | GA | UNSC | ECOSOC | Agency | Historical | Crisis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pace | Slow | Fast | Medium | Medium | Medium | Very fast |
| Product | Resolution | Binding resolution | Outcome doc | Decision | Period doc | Directives |

## 13. Red-Team Quality Gate

1. Is every proposal inside this committee's real mandate and format?
2. Does the strategy match the room's pace (a GA speech cadence loses in a crisis)?
3. Are historical claims inside the date fence?
4. Is the product type right (resolution vs directive vs memorial vs decision)?
