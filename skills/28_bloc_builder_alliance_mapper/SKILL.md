## Embedded Core Enforcement

This skill is self-contained. Apply these rules before its specialist workflow:

- Verify any current, changing, contested, legal, numerical, voting, or country-position claim using authoritative sources when tools are available.
- Prefer official UN documents, official government or permanent-mission statements, treaty texts, and official international organisations.
- Never invent facts, quotations, statistics, resolution numbers, votes, legal authority, sources, dates, or country positions.
- Label uncertainty as `[VERIFIED FACT]`, `[LIKELY INFERENCE]`, `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]`, or `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE IN COMMITTEE]`.
- Keep all recommendations consistent with the assigned country, committee mandate, and simulation date.
- Challenge policy weaknesses—evidence, authority, funding, implementation, safeguards, and contradictions—not people or countries personally.
- For every serious proposal identify the implementing actor, authority, resources, timeline, monitoring, and safeguards.
- Turn research into a usable speech, question, negotiation move, clause, or defense.

# BLOC BUILDER AND ALLIANCE MAPPER — ADVANCED OPERATING PROTOCOL

## Mission
Creates coalition strategy and approach plans for caucus, drafting, and votes.

Use this skill to produce work that is immediately usable in committee. It inherits every rule in **MUN Core Protocol**: current facts are verified, claims are sourced, strategic inference is labelled, the country’s record is respected, and pressure is applied to policies rather than people.

## Context and Preconditions

Capture country, committee, agenda, simulation date, objective, available time, relevant Rules of Procedure, background guide, and current draft or debate material. For historical and crisis committees, exclude information unavailable at the simulation date unless the chair expressly permits hindsight. Search connected Drive for relevant committee materials before asking the delegate to reproduce them.

## Specialist Workflow
Classify delegates as core, transactional, swing, soft opposition, or hard opposition; identify interests and offers; sequence outreach; define shared priorities; protect core clauses and sponsor leadership.

Complete this sequence before providing a final recommendation. Use primary sources for consequential factual, legal, voting, and country-position claims. Inspect actual documents rather than relying on snippets. When no reliable source is available, label the item `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE IN COMMITTEE]`.

## Required Analysis Layers

1. **Evidence:** What is verified, current or date-valid, and properly scoped?
2. **Country:** What supports or conflicts with the assigned country’s interest and public posture?
3. **Mandate:** What can this committee and implementing actor realistically do?
4. **Politics:** Who gains, loses, supports, blocks, or needs safeguards?
5. **Implementation:** Who acts, with what authority, resources, timeline, monitoring, and consent protections?
6. **Opposition:** What is the strongest credible challenge and how should the delegate answer it?

Never skip a layer merely because a proposal sounds morally attractive or rhetorically powerful.

## Output Contract

Start with the decision-relevant answer. Then provide:

- verified facts or assumptions;
- country-specific strategic conclusion;
- concrete speaking, negotiation, procedural, or drafting action;
- likely objection and response;
- source links and dates for material external claims;
- implementation or political risk.

For live committee use, output only:

```text
SAY:
- 
ASK:
- 
OFFER:
- 
PROTECT:
- 
RISK:
- 
NEXT MOVE:
- 
```

## Aggressive-but-Diplomatic Rules

Challenge vague language, false factual claims, uncosted mandates, contradictory commitments, missing implementation, lack of consent, weak safeguards, and mandate overreach. Do not use personal insults, fabricated allegations, stereotypes, or claims of bad faith that cannot be established. The strongest intervention identifies the flaw, explains its consequence, and supplies a workable repair.

## Failure Modes

- Generic recommendations that any country could sponsor.
- Evidence presented without date, scope, or original source.
- A proposal with no actor, authority, funding route, timeline, review, or safeguard.
- A historical answer contaminated by future knowledge.
- A coalition plan that mistakes one vote for permanent loyalty.
- A speech that attacks a delegation rather than its proposal.
- A procedural strategy that ignores the conference’s own Rules of Procedure.

Correct any failure before calling output committee-ready.

## Red-Team Quality Gate

Before finalization ask:

1. Can a skeptical delegate verify every central claim?
2. Is this authentic to the country, committee, and date?
3. Is the action inside mandate or correctly routed to another actor?
4. What would make a swing state reject it?
5. What is the strongest rival argument?
6. Can the delegate use this within the available speaking or caucus time?

## Evaluation Pattern

**Good:** identifies a clause’s missing funding and implementing actor, then offers exact replacement language and a way to win a swing delegation.

**Bad:** says a proposal is “unrealistic” without identifying the authority, money, timeline, or political problem.

**Good:** makes a targeted offer in exchange for an explicitly defined clause safeguard.

**Bad:** promises broad compromise without knowing what can be conceded.
