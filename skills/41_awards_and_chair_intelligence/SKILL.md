## Embedded Core Enforcement

This skill is self-contained. Apply these rules before its specialist workflow:

- Verify any current, changing, contested, legal, numerical, voting, or country-position claim using authoritative sources when tools are available.
- Prefer official UN documents, official government or permanent-mission statements, treaty texts, and official international organisations.
- Never invent facts, quotations, statistics, resolution numbers, votes, legal authority, sources, dates, or country positions.
- Label uncertainty as `[VERIFIED FACT]`, `[LIKELY INFERENCE]`, `[NEGOTIATION STRATEGY]`, or `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE IN COMMITTEE]`.
- Keep all recommendations consistent with the assigned country, committee mandate, and simulation date.
- Challenge policy weaknesses—evidence, authority, funding, implementation, safeguards, and contradictions—not people or countries personally.
- For every serious proposal identify the implementing actor, authority, resources, timeline, monitoring, and safeguards.
- Turn research into a usable speech, question, negotiation move, clause, or defense.

# AWARDS AND CHAIR INTELLIGENCE — OPERATING PROTOCOL

## Mission

Make the delegate the delegate the dais remembers — for the right reasons. Awards are not won by volume of speech. They are won by consistent, visible, constructive contribution across every phase of committee, tracked through the chair's lens.

## 1. What Chairs Actually Score

Across conferences the recurring criteria are:

1. **Knowledge** — of the rules, the topics, and the assigned country. Depth beats breadth; country-specific beats topic-generic.
2. **Diplomacy** — respectful, inclusive, solution-driven behaviour; building bridges between blocs; never attacking delegates.
3. **Procedure** — accurate use of motions and points, at the right moments.
4. **Leadership** — driving debate forward, drafting, amending, defending resolutions, handling crises.
5. **Constructiveness in caucuses** — chairs observe unmoderated caucuses closely. Idle delegates do not get considered.
6. **Resolution work** — the quality of what the delegate sponsored, signed, drafted, amended, or defended.
7. **Consistency** — contribution in every session, from opening speeches to final votes. Fading after session one is fatal.

Award tiers (typical): Best Delegate > Outstanding > Honorable Mention / Diplomacy Award > Best Position Paper > Verbal Commendation. Position papers are frequently a *gate* for delegate awards — a missing or weak paper disqualifies candidates at many conferences. Treat it as mandatory, not optional.

## 2. The Chair's Lens

Chairs watch three rooms at once: formal debate, unmoderated caucuses, and the paper trail. Build a record in all three:

- **Formal:** purposeful speeches that shift debate; clean procedural usage; questions that expose gaps rather than show off.
- **Caucus:** be the fixer — propose compromises, draft clauses, keep blocs working. Be visibly busy with substance, not gossip.
- **Paper trail:** your working paper, draft resolution, amendments, and position paper must be formatted correctly and well-written. Sloppy documents quietly sink candidates.

## 3. Delegate Persona

Five archetypes fill every committee: the aggressive talker, the quiet retreater, the sneaky influencer, the researcher, and the conflict mediator. The winning hybrid is **researcher + mediator + early leader**:

- **Early:** assert presence in the first session — the first 1–2 speeches set how the room classifies you.
- **Middle:** become the person consulted during unmods; the one who offers language, not just opinions.
- **Late:** be the anchor of a draft resolution and its calm defender.

Never be the loudest. One well-timed, insightful intervention that changes the frame beats five generic speeches.

## 4. Managing the Chair Relationship

- Address the dais correctly; follow procedure exactly — never misstate a rule.
- Be professional and punctual; respect dress code and conduct rules (they are scored).
- Do not challenge the chair needlessly; when you must, do it with a point of order, cleanly and once.
- Make the chair's job easier: propose clear motions with topic, time, and speaking time; volunteer to help organize drafts.
- Never deride other delegates or their resolutions — chairs weigh how you treat the room.

## 5. The Track Record Ledger

Keep a private contribution ledger during committee. After each session log:

- clauses drafted, amended, or defended (with which resolution);
- bridges built (bloc A and bloc B language merged);
- interventions that changed the frame or moved debate;
- procedural wins (motions passed that served the committee);
- crisis moves that were accepted by the backroom.

This ledger is also your self-audit: if a session has no entries, next session must.

## 6. Endgame Positioning

- Keep contributing to the final product; the last merger and the final defense matter as much as the opener.
- Close debate only when your whip count says you win (see Floor and Vote Strategist).
- In the final session, a short closing statement that frames the committee's achievement — crediting allies by name — leaves the strongest final impression.

## 7. Delegation-Level Awards

If the conference awards delegations, coordinate: consistent position-paper quality, unified standards, everyone attending all sessions, cross-committee intelligence sharing. One star plus five weak performances loses to six solid ones.

## 8. Anti-Patterns (each one quietly disqualifies)

- Loudest voice, no substance.
- Buzzwords and random statistics that deepen nothing.
- Derogatory comments about delegates or their papers.
- Idle during unmoderated caucuses.
- Broken character or inconsistent country stance.
- Position paper missing, late, or off-format.
- Procedural grandstanding — motions for attention, points for show.

## 9. Rapid Mode

```text
THIS SESSION'S SCORE TARGET:
- [one visible contribution: clause / bridge / frame shift]
CHAIR SIGNALS:
- [what the dais rewarded this session — adapt next session]
LEDGER:
- [drafted / amended / bridged / moved / crisis wins]
NEXT MOVE:
- [one action that builds the record]
```

## 10. Red-Team Quality Gate

1. If the chair summarized the delegate's contribution this session, what would they say?
2. Is the delegate visible in all three rooms (formal, caucus, paper)?
3. Is every central claim still verifiable and country-consistent?
4. Which competitor in the room is building a stronger record — and what are they doing that the delegate is not?
5. Does the endgame plan preserve the record built so far?
