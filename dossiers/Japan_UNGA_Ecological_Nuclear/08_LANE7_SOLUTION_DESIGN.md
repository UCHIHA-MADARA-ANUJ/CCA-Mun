# LANE 7 — SOLUTION DESIGN RESEARCH
## Research Planner Lane 7 · 3–5 leadable policy packages · FAMMS-tested · clause-ready

> Each package: problem it solves, mechanism, actor, authority, funding, timeline, monitoring, safeguards, political coalition, clause-ready text.

## P1 — Global Radioactive Legacy Remediation & Transparency Fund ("Legacy Fund")

### Problem solved
Contamination already in the ground and sea from 2,000+ tests and uranium mining — Runit Dome (cracking, no bottom lining, ~120,000 t), Semipalatinsk (456 tests), Polynesia (193), Central Asia tailings, Arctic dumping, Punggye-ri collapse risk — with no dedicated international remediation vehicle.

### Mechanism
- GA requests the Secretary-General, with IAEA + UNEP, to establish a voluntary multi-donor **Global Radioactive Legacy Remediation and Transparency Fund**.
- Phase 1 (24 months): independent baseline surveys of legacy sites (host-state consent).
- Phase 2: remediation pilot projects — **Runit Dome (Marshall Islands)** and **Semipalatinsk/Central Asia tailings (Kazakhstan)** named as first pilots.
- Public data portal: survey results, radionuclide measurements, funding flows, project status.
- 5-year independent review.

### FAMMS
- **F**easible: voluntary fund (Japan, EU, US, Gulf donors), pilot-first, existing agencies execute.
- **A**uthority: GA can request/establish via SG; IAEA/UNEP operate within their mandates.
- **M**easurable: baseline surveys, published radionuclide data, funding disclosure, review reports.
- **M**oney: voluntary contributions; Japan signals leadership with an opening pledge (verify amount rules with chair).
- **S**afeguards: host-state consent; IAEA safety standards; independent monitoring; transparency portal.

### Political coalition
Marshall Islands + Kazakhstan + Pacific + Central Asia (moral core, co-authors); US/EU/Japan (donors); nobody can vote against "clean up the contamination" — even China/Russia abstain rather than say no.

### Clause-ready text
> "1. Requests the Secretary-General, in cooperation with the International Atomic Energy Agency and the United Nations Environment Programme, to establish a voluntary Global Radioactive Legacy Remediation and Transparency Fund to support baseline environmental surveys and remediation of legacy nuclear-test and uranium-mining sites, beginning with pilot projects in the Marshall Islands and Central Asia, subject to the consent of the host States concerned;
> 2. Requests the Fund to publish, through an open data portal, survey findings, radionuclide monitoring data, funding contributions and project implementation status, with an independent review to be submitted to the General Assembly within five years;"

## P2 — Transboundary Radiation Monitoring & Early Notification Protocol ("No-Borders Protocol")

### Problem solved
Fallout and contamination ignore borders (Lucky Dragon 1954; Fukushima data crossing the Pacific faster than governments shared it; Runit groundwater pumping into the lagoon). The 1986 Early Notification Convention covers accidents, not legacy sites and testing risks, and has no open-data layer.

### Mechanism
- GA requests IAEA + CTBTO, with member states, to establish an **open transboundary radiation monitoring data portal** — building on the CTBTO IMS (337 stations) and national networks.
- States encouraged to share real-time data on a voluntary basis; data sovereignty preserved.
- Regional joint-response exercises, beginning in the Asia-Pacific; Japan offers to host the first.
- Annual reporting to the GA.

### FAMMS
- **F**easible: IMS exists; national networks exist; cost is coordination, not construction.
- **A**uthority: IAEA/CTBTO within mandates; GA requests.
- **M**easurable: stations contributing data, exercises held, portal traffic/coverage, reports.
- **M**oney: modest voluntary contributions; Japan + Australia + ROK as first donors.
- **S**afeguards: national control of national data; open access for science; no surveillance language.

### Political coalition
Japan (author), Pacific islands (co-authors — their own demand), Australia, ROK, Nordics; Russia/China cannot publicly oppose "better monitoring."

### Clause-ready text
> "3. Requests the International Atomic Energy Agency and the Preparatory Commission for the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization, in cooperation with Member States, to establish an open transboundary radiation monitoring data portal building on the International Monitoring System and national monitoring networks, and to support regional joint emergency-response exercises, beginning in the Asia-Pacific region;
> 4. Encourages all States to share radiation monitoring data on a voluntary basis and in accordance with national laws, and invites the Secretary-General to report annually on the operation of the portal;"

## P3 — Testing Taboo & Environmental Impact Assessment ("No More Lucky Dragons")

### Problem solved
The moratorium that held since 1996 is under pressure (US testing-order rhetoric Oct 2025 — clarified as subcritical; Russia revoked CTBT ratification Nov 2023; DPRK site activity; CTBT still not in force). Testing has no environmental-assessment requirement, and the worst contamination in history came from tests (530 atmospheric).

### Mechanism
- GA calls upon all states to **reaffirm the moratorium on nuclear test explosions**.
- GA requests that **any future nuclear test explosion be preceded by a published, independent environmental impact assessment** conducted to international standards, with results transmitted to the Secretary-General.
- GA calls for **CTBT entry into force** and welcomes the CTBTO's verification work.
- GA supports the **NPT intersessional process** on the humanitarian and environmental dimensions, building on the 2026 RevCon's retained "environmental remediation, affected communities" language.

### FAMMS
- **F**easible: political commitments; no new machinery required (EIA can use IAEA/UNEP standards).
- **A**uthority: GA recommendation; consistent with NPT Art. VI and the 1996 ICJ Opinion.
- **M**easurable: number of states reaffirming; ratifications; EIA reports if any.
- **M**oney: minimal (CTBTO support).
- **S**afeguards: EIA is an assessment, not a veto — framed so it cannot be read as interfering with security decisions.

### Political coalition
Japan (author), moral core, TPNW bloc (they want the taboo strongest), UK/France/Australia (CTBT supporters). US is the sensitive one — universal language + "in accordance with national circumstances" softener ready as compromise.

### Clause-ready text
> "5. Calls upon all States to reaffirm the moratorium on nuclear test explosions and to refrain from any action that would undermine it;
> 6. Requests that any future nuclear test explosion be preceded by a published independent environmental impact assessment, conducted in accordance with internationally recognized standards and in accordance with national circumstances, with the results transmitted to the Secretary-General;
> 7. Calls upon all States that have not yet done so to sign and ratify the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty without delay, and welcomes the work of the Preparatory Commission for the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization;"

## P4 — Feed the UN Scientific Panel (cheap consensus multiplier)

### Problem solved
The GA-established 21-member Scientific Panel on the Effects of Nuclear War (report due 2027) is exactly this agenda's evidence engine — but its scope and data flows can be strengthened, and the committee can claim ownership of the process.

### Mechanism
- GA welcomes the establishment of the Scientific Panel (resolution of Dec 2024).
- Requests the Panel to include **legacy nuclear-test contamination and transboundary radiation impacts** among its case studies.
- Encourages states to provide data, host regional meetings, and contribute.
- Invites the Panel's interim findings for the committee's future work.

### Political caution
France, UK, Russia voted against the Panel resolution; US abstained. Keep this clause as "welcomes/already established," never "establish a new study." The 136-3-29 vote means most of the room supports it.

### Clause-ready text
> "8. Welcomes the establishment of the United Nations Scientific Panel on the Effects of Nuclear War and requests that its work include case studies on the environmental consequences of nuclear testing and on transboundary radiation impacts, and encourages Member States to provide relevant data and to facilitate regional meetings of the Panel;"

## P5 — Recognition, Education & Capacity (the consensus glue)

### Mechanism
- Recognition of affected communities and legacy sites (mirrors NPT Rev.4 retained language).
- Disarmament and non-proliferation education (Japan's flagship joint statement; 88 supporters in 2022).
- Technical assistance and capacity-building for affected developing states (monitoring equipment, training).
- Peaceful-uses reassurance (NPT Art. IV) — no proliferation pressure read as energy discrimination.

### Clause-ready text
> "9. Recognizes the affected communities of past nuclear testing and uranium mining, including in the Pacific, Central Asia and beyond, and affirms the importance of environmental remediation, affected-community health, and disarmament and non-proliferation education;
> 10. Invites States, relevant United Nations entities and civil society to contribute to technical assistance and capacity-building for affected developing States, including monitoring equipment, training and scientific exchange, and reaffirms the inalienable right of States parties to the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons to the peaceful uses of nuclear energy in accordance with its Article IV;"
