## 4.1 The Treaties

### NPT — Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (1968, in force 1970)
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] 191 states parties (Palestine last to accede, 2015); DPRK's withdrawal (2003) legally contested.
- **Three pillars:** non-proliferation (Art. II), peaceful uses (Art. IV), disarmament (Art. VI — "pursue negotiations in good faith on effective measures relating to cessation of the nuclear arms race at an early date and to nuclear disarmament").
- **Relevance:** the regime Japan defends; the 2026 RevCon's failure is the backdrop of the whole committee.
- **Gap:** the NPT has no environmental/ecological article — the treaty never addresses contamination, legacy sites, or transboundary radiation. **Drafting opportunity:** Japan's resolution supplies "the environmental clause the NPT lacks."
- **Non-parties:** India, Pakistan, Israel (never joined); DPRK (withdrew). Use "calls upon all states" for them, not NPT-party language.

### CTBT — Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (opened Sep 24, 1996)
- [VERIFIED FACT] 178 ratifications; NOT in force; requires ratification by 44 Annex-2 states; outstanding: China, Egypt, India, Iran, Israel, DPRK, Pakistan, US (8) — plus Russia revoked its ratification Nov 2, 2023 (it had ratified 2000).
- [VERIFIED FACT] The CTBTO's International Monitoring System (IMS): 337 stations worldwide (seismic, hydroacoustic, infrasound, radionuclide) providing near-real-time data; since the treaty is not in force, CTBTO lacks formal authority to "confirm" a test (CTBTO FAQ).
- **Relevance:** the testing taboo's legal instrument; entry into force is a standard Japan clause ("calls upon all states to sign and ratify").
- **Gap:** no environmental-assessment requirement for tests. **Drafting opportunity:** the EIA-before-test clause.

### TPNW — Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (adopted Jul 7, 2017, in force Jan 22, 2021)
- [VERIFIED FACT] 75 states parties, ~95–100 signatories (as of Jul 2026; Tonga most recent); adoption vote 122-1-1; no nuclear-armed state is a party; US/UK/France joint statement: will "never become party" (Wikipedia/PSR, Jul 2026).
- **Relevance:** the rival path. Japan not a party; votes against the annual UNGA TPNW-welcoming resolution since 2018.
- **Gap:** TPNW parties = the "affected communities" moral bloc; Japan cannot join but must not alienate. **Drafting opportunity:** recognizing language ("recognizing the TPNW as an expression of the shared goal of a world without nuclear weapons" — mirrors Japan's own 2023 formulation).

### 1986 Conventions (post-Chernobyl) — the direct precedent for Japan's Protocol
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Convention on Early Notification of a Nuclear Accident (IAEA, adopted Sep 26, 1986): states must notify and provide information on any nuclear accident with transboundary radiological significance.
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Convention on Assistance in the Case of a Nuclear Accident or Radiological Emergency: framework for international assistance.
- **Relevance:** Japan's "Transboundary Radiation Monitoring & Early Notification Protocol" extends these instruments to data-sharing and testing-site risk. **Drafting opportunity:** "calls upon states parties to implement fully, invites accession" + "requests IAEA to develop standard formats for real-time data sharing."

### Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zones — the proven regional architecture
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Tlatelolco (Latin America, 1967), Rarotonga (South Pacific, 1985), Bangkok (SE Asia, 1995), Pelindaba (Africa, 1996), CANWFZ (Central Asia, 2006, in force 2009 — only NWFZ in the Northern Hemisphere), Mongolia (single-state).
- [VERIFIED FACT] CANWFZ: signed Sep 8, 2006 at Semipalatinsk; entered into force Mar 2009; Protocol (negative security assurances) ratified by UK, China, Russia, France (Astana Times, 2021–22).
- **Relevance:** zones are the existing "global boundaries" architecture — radiation-safe regions. **Drafting opportunity:** "welcomes the nuclear-weapon-free zones as confidence-building frameworks and encourages the extension of their monitoring principles."

### Maritime instruments
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] UNCLOS Part XII (Protection and Preservation of the Marine Environment) — states must prevent, reduce, and control pollution of the marine environment (Art. 194).
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] London Convention (1972): bans dumping of high-level radioactive waste at sea (1993 amendment); London Protocol (1996): bans all sea dumping (in force 2006).
- **Relevance:** legal basis for the "maritime radioactive legacy sites" clause (sunken vessels like K-278 *Komsomolets*, historical dumping grounds). **Drafting opportunity:** clause 7 of the draft skeleton.

### Environmental law principles
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Stockholm Declaration Principle 21 (1972) / Rio Declaration Principle 2 (1992): states have responsibility to ensure activities within their jurisdiction do not cause damage to the environment of other states or areas beyond national jurisdiction.
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Trail Smelter arbitration (1941) — foundational "no transboundary harm" case.
- **Relevance:** the legal spine of "radioactivity does not recognize borders."

## 4.2 ICJ Jurisprudence

### ICJ Advisory Opinion, *Legality of the Threat or Use of Nuclear Weapons* (July 8, 1996)
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Threat/use of nuclear weapons "would generally be contrary to the rules of international law applicable in armed conflict, in particular the principles and rules of humanitarian law"; but the Court could not conclude definitively whether it would be lawful in extreme self-defense (survival of the state). Also: "there exists an obligation to pursue in good faith and bring to a conclusion negotiations leading to nuclear disarmament in all its aspects under strict and effective international control" (unanimous).
- **Relevance:** the disarmament-obligation anchor; safe to "recall."

### Marshall Islands v. UK, India, Pakistan (2014–2016)
- [VERIFIED FACT] Marshall Islands filed against all nine nuclear-armed states (Apr 2014); ICJ dismissed the remaining cases (UK: 8-8, President's casting vote; India; Pakistan) on Oct 5, 2016 — lack of jurisdiction (no "dispute" established), never reached merits; President Abraham noted RMI's interest "by virtue of the suffering of its people" (DW, Oct 5, 2016; Harvard ILJ, Nov 2016).
- **Relevance:** affected states tried the court and were turned away on a technicality — the political route (this committee) is the only route left. Use to recruit Marshall Islands.
