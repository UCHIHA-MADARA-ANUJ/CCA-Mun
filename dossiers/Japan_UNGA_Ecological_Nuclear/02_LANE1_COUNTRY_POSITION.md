# LANE 1 — COUNTRY POSITION RESEARCH: JAPAN
## Research Planner Lane 1 · Country Position · Aug 2026 committee date

> Purpose: Japan's public stance, interests, red lines, negotiating room, and voting record on nuclear disarmament and ecological issues. Every claim labeled and dated. Sources: MOFA, permanent mission statements, official statements, voting records, IAEA, AEC.

## 1.1 The Doctrine in One Line

**Japan pursues "a world without nuclear weapons" through the NPT and incremental, verifiable steps — not through the TPNW — and carries the moral authority of the only nation to suffer atomic bombing.**

---

## 1.2 Verified Position Pillars

### P1 — The hibakusha identity is Japan's foundation
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan is the only country to have suffered atomic bombing: Hiroshima (Aug 6, 1945) and Nagasaki (Aug 9, 1945).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Nihon Hidankyo (Japan Confederation of A- and H-Bomb Sufferers Organizations) won the 2024 Nobel Peace Prize "for its efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons and for demonstrating through witness testimony that nuclear weapons must never be used again" (Nobel Committee citation; Reuters, Oct 11, 2024).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan's 2024 annual UNGA resolution (31st consecutive) explicitly referenced Nihon Hidankyo's Nobel Prize and called on states to promote interactions with hibakusha (Japan Times, Nov 2, 2024).
- **Committee use:** the opening and closing of every Japan speech. Opponents cannot attack hibakusha without losing the room.

### P2 — The NPT is "the cornerstone"; the TPNW is not Japan's path
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan's official line (FM Hayashi, Aug 2022): "The NPT is the cornerstone of the international nuclear disarmament and non-proliferation regime… maintaining and strengthening the NPT is the only realistic path toward a world without nuclear weapons" (MOFA press release, Aug 27, 2022).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan did not participate in TPNW negotiations (2017), is not a party, and has voted against the annual UNGA resolution welcoming the TPNW since 2018 (ICANW Japan profile, 2023).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan's official 2023 formulation: "The TPNW is an important treaty that could be regarded as a final passage to a world without nuclear weapons. However, in order to change the reality, engagement with the nuclear-weapon states is necessary" (Japan statement, via ICANW).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan's constitutional/identity anchor: the Three Non-Nuclear Principles (1967, PM Eisaku Sato): not to possess, not to manufacture, not to permit introduction of nuclear weapons. Sato won the 1974 Nobel Peace Prize (Reuters, Oct 2024).
- **Committee use:** when a TPNW bloc pushes maximalism, Japan's line is: "We respect the TPNW as a legitimate path; our resolution is the bridge both paths can sign today." Never criticize the TPNW itself — praise it and explain why Japan's lane is engagement.

### P3 — Japan's annual flagship resolution: "United action with renewed determination towards the total elimination of nuclear weapons"
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan has submitted this resolution to the UNGA for 31+ consecutive years (2024 was the 31st; Japan Times, Nov 2, 2024).
- [VERIFIED FACT] First Committee vote (Nov 1, 2024): 145 in favor, 6 against (China, Russia, DPRK, Iran, Nicaragua, Syria), 28 abstentions (France, India, Israel among abstainers); US and UK vote in favor (Japan Times, Nov 2, 2024).
- [VERIFIED FACT] GA plenary vote (Dec 2, 2024): 152-6-28 (Japan Times, Dec 3, 2024).
- [VERIFIED FACT] 2025 (32nd year): GA plenary 147-5-26; First Committee 145-5-28 (MOFA press release, Dec 2, 2025).
- [VERIFIED FACT] The 2024 resolution: (a) notes "achieving a world without nuclear weapons is a common goal for the international community"; (b) calls for promoting interactions with hibakusha; (c) expresses deep concern that actions against Ukraine's sovereignty "make the threat of nuclear weapons use today higher than at any time since the heights of the Cold War"; (d) clarifies DPRK "cannot and will never have the status of a nuclear-weapon state" under the NPT (Japan Times, Nov 2, 2024).
- **Committee use:** the room's arithmetic on ANY disarmament resolution will mirror this: ~145-152 yes, China/Russia/DPRK/Iran/Syria/Nicaragua no, France/India/Israel abstain. Japan is the natural lead author; the US votes WITH Japan on this.

### P4 — Disarmament and non-proliferation education (Japan's signature side-issue)
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan submits a Joint Statement on Disarmament and Non-Proliferation Education; 88 countries joined in 2022 — the largest support ever (MOFA, FM Hayashi statement, Aug 2022).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Japan again delivered the joint statement at the 2026 NPT Review Conference (Ambassador Ichikawa, Main Committee I, April 30, 2026) (Delegation of Japan to the CD website).
- **Committee use:** cheap, consensus-safe clause ("calls upon states to promote disarmament and non-proliferation education") that brings the TPNW bloc and NAM in without cost.

### P5 — The 2026 NPT RevCon: Japan's official voice
- [VERIFIED FACT] State Minister for Foreign Affairs KUNIMITSU Ayano, General Debate, April 28, 2026: the RevCon convenes amid "an increasingly severe international security situation, an opaque and rapid expansion of nuclear arsenals, and growing nuclear risks and threats"; "at the very origin of our aspiration for a world without nuclear weapons lies the earnest plea of the hibakusha"; Japan has "consistently pursued the path of a peace-loving nation since the end of World War II"; calls for "rebuilding trust among nations, even if incrementally" (Statement, Japan Mission to the UN, Apr 28, 2026).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Ambassador Ichikawa spoke at Main Committee I (May 5, 2026), Subsidiary Bodies 1 and 3 (May 6, 2026), and the Closing session (May 22, 2026) (Delegation of Japan to the CD website).
- **Committee use:** Japan's official framing at the failed RevCon = "rebuild trust incrementally" + "opaque and rapid expansion of nuclear arsenals" (China-coded) + hibakusha. Quote Kunimitsu in your GSL — it is your government's own line.

### P6 — Japan's national record (defensive and environmental)
- [VERIFIED FACT] Separated plutonium: ~44.4 tons total at end-2024 (8.6 t domestic, 35.8 t abroad); zero consumption, zero new recovery in 2024; published under the Plutonium Management Guidelines (Japan Atomic Energy Commission Status Report, Aug 5, 2025).
- [VERIFIED FACT] Fukushima Daiichi: ALPS-treated water discharges began Aug 2023; ~125,400 m³ released through 17th batch (Dec 2025); IAEA confirms tritium far below the 1,500 Bq/L operational limit, "in line with international safety standards"; IAEA comprehensive review (July 4, 2023) found the plan consistent with international safety standards with "negligible radiological impact" (IAEA press releases; ANS, Dec 11, 2025).
- [VERIFIED FACT] *Defense of Japan 2026* (released Aug 4, 2026 — 17 days before committee): China = Japan's "greatest strategic challenge ever"; China "consistently increased military spending without transparency and has rapidly and extensively strengthened its nuclear and missile capabilities"; PM Sanae Takaichi; Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi (India Today, Aug 5, 2026; The Diplomat, Aug 4, 2026).
- [CONTEXTUAL FACT] Japan is the third-largest contributor to the UN regular budget (~10-11%) — a major funder of UN disarmament machinery (UN scale of assessments).

## 1.3 Interests Hierarchy (what Japan actually wants in this committee)

1. **Protect moral authority** — the hibakusha narrative is Japan's soft-power crown jewel; every proposal should be framed as "the hibakusha demand."
2. **Keep the NPT alive** after the third consecutive RevCon failure — Japan's resolution should be read as "the NPT's environmental clause."
3. **Pin down China and DPRK on transparency/verification** — ecological framing is Japan's diplomatic cover for security concerns.
4. **Build the Pacific coalition** — Japan needs Marshall Islands + Pacific islands for moral legitimacy; the Fukushima dispute makes this urgent.
5. **Defend Fukushima/ALPS as IAEA-verified** — pre-empt the Pacific attack.
6. **Not be forced into TPNW** — maintain the umbrella; avoid a vote that isolates Japan from US.

## 1.4 Red Lines (non-negotiable)

| # | Red line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | No language obligating Japan to sign the TPNW or abandon extended deterrence | Alliance + domestic politics |
| R2 | No clause legitimizing or normalizing any nuclear test | Core principle |
| R3 | No singling-out of Japan on civil nuclear energy | Agenda is about proliferation/weapons |
| R4 | No specific reparations figures naming Japan as payer | Japan is not the responsible party; channel via multilateral funds |

## 1.5 Tradeable Items (negotiating capital)

- [NEGOTIATION STRATEGY] Support "recognition of affected communities and legacy sites" language (costs nothing; wins Pacific + Kazakhstan).
- [NEGOTIATION STRATEGY] Support "disarmament education" and "youth/hibakusha testimony" clauses (Japan's own flagship; brings TPNW bloc in).
- [NEGOTIATION STRATEGY] Accept "capacity-building and technical assistance" for developing states (Japan funds these anyway).
- [NEGOTIATION STRATEGY] Accept softer language on "humanitarian and environmental dimensions" (mirrors Rev.4 retained text) in exchange for hard mechanisms on monitoring/remediation.
