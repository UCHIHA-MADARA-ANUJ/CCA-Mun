# SECTION 16 — Q&A WAR ROOM + GOTCHA DEFENSE (the 12 most dangerous questions)

Format: **Q → why dangerous → BEST answer → redirect**. (Short/diplomatic/aggressive variants implied by tone scaling; if you genuinely don't know: *"The delegation will verify against the IAEA/UN record and return to the floor with the source"* — never bluff a number.)

**1. "If Hiroshima is your soul, why won't Japan sign the TPNW?"** ★most-likely
→ *Danger:* moral decapitation; splits you from abolition bloc.
→ *Best:* "Because Japan's mission is a world without nuclear weapons that *actually arrives*. The TPNW binds the states that already renounced the bomb and changes the arsenals of none; it has no verification machinery and sits outside the NPT architecture on which global restraint still rests. Japan's path — the resolutions that carry 152 to 158 nations *including London, Paris and Washington* `[VERIFIED]` — keeps the weapons-states inside the room where verification, risk-reduction and disarmament can be negotiated, not merely proclaimed. And in this committee, the consequence is practical: this text, Japan-led, can pass with the states whose behavior must change. A letter they will not sign shields no community."
→ *Redirect:* "Will the distinguished delegate co-architect the remediation pillar of a text that can pass — or settle for one that cannot?"

**2. "You lecture on ecology while discharging Fukushima water into the Pacific. Why are you credible?"**
→ *Danger:* China + PIF amplification; agenda hijack to ALPS.
→ *Best:* "Credibility is precisely what Japan established — not by assertion, but by invitation. The IAEA's comprehensive review of July 2023 found the discharge plan consistent with international safety standards and of negligible radiological impact; the IAEA has maintained an office at the site since; monitoring data is public, continuous and independently corroborated; and the framework was **expanded so that Chinese experts participate in sampling** — following which Beijing began adjusting its import measures `[VERIFIED — AP/IAEA 2023–24]`. That is the model this resolution generalizes: when an ecological question crosses a boundary, answer it with verification, not volume."
→ *Redirect:* "Does the delegate accept IAEA verification as the standard for every legacy site — including the one their own region has never opened?"

**3. "Aren't you a nuclear-umbrella state pretending to be a peace state?"**
→ *Danger:* Russia/DPRK + abolitionist chorus.
→ *Best:* "Japan is a state with Three Non-Nuclear Principles in its national identity — possessing, producing and permitting none — living beside the region's fastest nuclear build-ups. Extended deterrence is a defensive security fact; disarmament is our permanent destination; and between fact and destination stands the unglamorous work Japan actually does: verification funding, education, and the only UN texts that unite the P3 and the non-nuclear majority — most recently on the record at this year's Review Conference, where Japan fought to keep both Korean-peninsula and nuclear-safety language alive `[VERIFIED]`."
→ *Redirect:* "Judge delegations by what their texts build. Ours builds assessment; what does the attacker's build?"

**4. "Your plutonium stockpile and Rokkasho — latent bomb capability? Ecological risk?"**
→ *Danger:* non-proliferation purists + China.
→ *Best:* "Japan's entire program operates under full-scope IAEA safeguards with all holdings declared; national policy bars plutonium use expansion without purpose; and the pairing of a transparent civilian cycle with the Three Non-Nuclear Principles is precisely why Japan's non-proliferation credibility is structurally different from the opaque programs this committee should worry about. `[VERIFICATION FLAG: carry current stockpile figure from IAEA/Japan annual plutonium management report]`"
→ *Redirect:* "Opaque stocks, not safeguarded ones, are the proliferation-ecology problem."

**5. "Isn't Fukushima itself proof that nuclear technology is an ecological threat — will you admit liability framing?"**
→ *Danger:* V5 flip; pushes 'nuclear = ecological threat' category collapse.
→ *Best:* "Fukushima is proof of what Japan did about the aftermath: an independent regulator, the world's most transparent decommissioning, and safety standards exported worldwide through the IAEA. Experience made Japan the strictest student of nuclear safety — which is why this committee's ecological work is safest in the hands of those who have paid for the lesson."
→ *Redirect:* "The question is not whether to learn from 2011, but whether the world institutionalizes the learning — that is OP1."

**6. "Why is Japan restarting reactors while preaching ecological caution?"** ★likely (Kashiwazaki Feb 2026 is fresh)
→ *Best:* "Because decarbonization without blackouts is an ecological obligation too. Japan's restarts occur only under its independent post-Fukushima regulator; the Seventh Energy Plan pairs ~20% nuclear by 2040 with renewables expansion `[VERIFIED]`. This committee addresses the weapons enterprise; civilian power under IAEA safeguards is a different file — deliberately."
→ *Redirect:* point to OP7 — "this text says exactly that."

**7. "Assessment is a delay tactic. Why not immediate disarmament obligations?"**
→ *Danger:* abolitionist energy; makes you the "process" dullard.
→ *Best:* "Assessment is what makes action irreversible: remediation needs baselines, verification needs baselines, liability needs baselines. Every successful environmental regime — ozone, shipping, pollution — began with measurement architecture. Meanwhile the 'immediate' path produced three dead Review Conferences. Japan chooses the machinery that survives failure, not the declarations that produce it."
→ *Redirect:* "Name one remediation programme that succeeded without assessment. We will co-sponsor it tomorrow."

**8. "Why should the P5 tolerate your moratorium reaffirmation when the US ordered testing readiness?"**
→ *Danger:* paints you as anti-ally; DPRK/Russia delight.
→ *Best:* "Because the moratorium era was the P5's own achievement — the IMS is substantially American-built engineering; the zero-yield norm survived every administra­tion's review because stockpile stewardship works without detonations `[VERIFIED — NAS/DOE stewardship record]`. Our text names no state and protects every ocean."
→ *Redirect:* "The clause defends everyone's fish. Why would anyone oppose it?"

**9. "Marshal Islands/Kiribati delegate: Will Japan fund our remediation, concretely?"**
→ *Danger:* get trapped into dollar promises.
→ *Best:* "Japan has funded Pacific resilience, health and environmental programming for decades and designs OP5's trust fund to be contribution-open and priority-first for legacy sites `[CONTEXTUAL]`. → *Best:* "The GA cannot adjudicate liability — that is why hard texts die 170-to-4 every year while sites stay poisoned.
