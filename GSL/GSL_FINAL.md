## THE SPEECH

[SLOW — the hook]
Some speeches before mine offered words: *deeply concerned*, cooperation, understanding.

Japan is not here to add one more number for those words.

Concern costs nothing. It always has. The dead paid the price.

Words are cheap in this chamber — they always have been. Japan has stopped buying them.

[BEAT]
Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates.

[FAST — the record, one fact per breath]
Eighty-one years ago, an atomic bomb destroyed Hiroshima. Days later, Nagasaki. Two hundred thousand dead. This year, fewer survivors than ever.
The treaty between the largest arsenals expired in February. No successor. No negotiations.
The NPT failed in May. Again.
An army occupies Europe's largest nuclear plant — one wire from the point of no return.
The fastest buildup since the Cold War grows in the dark. No count. No answers. Twelve thousand warheads.
One state in this room wrote "irreversible" into law — and its missiles have flown over Japan.

[HARD — snap one]
When this Assembly was asked to help the poisoned, four states voted no. Three years in a row. The record is public. *THEIR NAMES ARE IN IT.*

[SLOW]
Japan's witnesses — the hibakusha — average eighty-seven. Their testimony will not outlast this deliberation.

[BEAT]
Japan's answer is machinery — monitoring, warning, remediation.

[CLIMB — the crescendo, rise line by line]
Hiroshima was the first city the bomb destroyed. Eighty-one years, the world promised Nagasaki last.
Every year — the arsenals grew.
Every year — the promise broke.
[HARD — the peak, full voice]
A vow broken for eighty-one years is not a vow. *IT IS A LIE.*
*ENOUGH.* This committee ends the lie.

[SLOW]
Radiation has never respected a border. In 1954 it poisoned Japanese fishermen who were never told a bomb was coming.

[HARD — the choice, flat and cold]
Japan offers what the speeches did not: a vote for the machinery.
Vote with us — your name beside the survivors.
Vote against us — your name beside the four.
[SHOUT — the final peak]
The record is written tonight. *WE WILL REMEMBER WHO WAS HERE — AND WHO WAS NOT.*

[SOFT]
I yield to points of information.

*(Hold one second of silence. Sit. No smile.)*

## DELIVERY — read this BEFORE you practice

- **Loud room = your weapon is the staircase.** The crescendo is a ladder: rise through "Every year — the arsenals grew. Every year — the promise broke," peak at "IT IS A LIE" and "ENOUGH" at full voice, then **drop cold** into "Radiation has never respected a border." The fall after the shout is the silence that wins rooms.
- **[FAST]** = clipped, driving, one fact per breath, hands cutting the air once per line.
- **[CLIMB]** = each phrase louder; peak at full power.
- **[HARD]** = strike the first word, then a full beat of silence.
- **[SLOW]** = cold, flat. Rage without volume is more frightening than rage with it.
- "One state in this room" — do not look at anyone. Let the room turn by itself.
- "THEIR NAMES ARE IN IT" — first snap. Full voice, then silence.
- **The choice-closer:** deliver flat and cold — "Vote with us... Vote against us..." — then explode on the final line: "WE WILL REMEMBER WHO WAS HERE — AND WHO WAS NOT." Let that land for a full second before the soft yield.
- **Yield line:** flat, faint smile. Hold. Someone WILL rise. Answer with the record.
- Pace: 170-175 wpm. Rehearse with a timer. Cut ladder is your safety net, not your plan.
