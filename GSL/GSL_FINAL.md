## THE SPEECH

[SLOW]
Five speeches before mine offered this committee three words: dialogue, cooperation, understanding.
[BEAT]
Japan has heard those words for eighty-one years. We are done hearing them.

[BEAT]
Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates.

[FAST — climb the ladder, no gap between counts]
Look at this record. A treaty between the two largest arsenals expired in February. No successor. No talks. No shame.
The treaty meant to govern this system failed its review in May — again.
An army occupies the largest nuclear plant in Europe — on a single power line. One shell — and the wind carries the rest.
The fastest buildup since the Cold War grows behind closed doors. No count. No answers.
One member of this committee declared its arsenal "irreversible" — and flies missiles over my country.

[HARD]
When this Assembly was asked to help the poisoned — four flags voted no. Three years running.
[SLOW] They know who they are. [HARD] The record knows their names. So do we.

[BEAT]
Japan does not lecture from a library. We buried two atomic cities. Our survivors — the last of them — average eighty-seven. They do not have time for another round of dialogue.

Our answer is not a speech. It is machinery: shared radiation monitoring. Early warning across borders. Cleanup of poisoned land.
[CLIMB]
We will not ask permission to measure. To warn. To heal what the arsenals broke.

[HARD] [SLOW]
Hiroshima was the first city destroyed by the bomb. For eighty years, this Assembly swore Nagasaki would be the last. The arsenals grew anyway. A vow broken for eighty years is not a vow — it is a habit.
[BEAT]
This room ends the habit.

[SLOW]
Radiation never asked for a passport. We keep pretending it needs a visa.

[HARD]
Japan is done asking. We are here to build. Anyone who prefers excuses to machinery — stand up. Say it to the record.

[SOFT]
I yield to points of information.

*(Hold one second of silence. Sit. No smile.)*
