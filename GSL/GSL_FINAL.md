# GSL FINAL — THE RECORD

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Agenda:** Assessing the ecological threats of nuclear proliferation on global boundaries  
**Speaking time:** 90 seconds  
**Speech length:** 222 written words; built for approximately 84 seconds of speech plus six seconds of deliberate silence  
**Status:** Final, primary-source fact audit · 21 August 2026

---

## SPEECH

[COLD]

Deeply concerned. Cooperation. Understanding.

For eighty-one years, the vocabulary changed. Nuclear weapons survived every sentence.

Japan will not offer a fourth.

Atomic bombs devastated Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945. By year’s end, municipal estimates put the death toll above two hundred thousand. Japan’s certified hibakusha now average nearly eighty-seven.

[ACCELERATE — one fact per breath]

New START expired in February—no legally binding successor.

The May NPT Review Conference ended without consensus—for the third consecutive time.

Military activity continues near Europe’s largest nuclear power plant.

SIPRI estimates twelve thousand one hundred and eighty-seven nuclear weapons.

One State here put its nuclear policy in its Constitution—and its ballistic missiles have flown over Japan.

For three consecutive years, this Assembly voted on victim assistance and environmental remediation. The same four States voted no each time.

[HARD]

**THE RECORD IS PUBLIC. THEIR NAMES ARE IN IT.**

[CONTROLLED]

Japan offers machinery: shared radiation monitoring, warning across borders, and remediation of contaminated land.

In 1954, fallout irradiated all twenty-three Lucky Dragon crew outside the declared danger zone.

[HARD]

**RADIATION CROSSED THE LINE. RESPONSIBILITY DID NOT.**

[CLIMB]

Hiroshima was the first city attacked with an atomic bomb. Whether Nagasaki remains the last depends on what this room builds.

Vote for machinery—your name beside the survivors.

Vote against it—your name beside the four.

[FINAL PEAK]

**EIGHTY-ONE YEARS OF WORDS ARE ENOUGH.**

[QUIET]

I yield to points of information.

## ABSOLUTE FACT-CHECK VERDICT

Every checkable spoken claim is either:

- **FACT:** supported by an official or high-quality source;
- **ATTRIBUTED ESTIMATE:** expressly identified as an estimate; or
- **RHETORIC / POLICY:** not presented as an empirical fact.

No bloc name, member count, sponsor hint, Prime Minister quotation, operational secret, exact unofficial national arsenal count, future real-world UN meeting, or formal greeting appears in the speech.

### Line-by-line audit

| Spoken line or claim | Classification | Verification and safe scope |
|---|---|---|
| “For eighty-one years…” | FACT + rhetoric | August 1945 to August 2026 is 81 years. The sentence does not falsely claim that the UN General Assembly itself has met for 81 complete years. |

| Certified hibakusha average nearly 87 | FACT, dated | Japan’s Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare reported 91,105 certified hibakusha at the end of March 2026, with an average age of 86.66. “Nearly eighty-seven” is exact rounding. |
| New START expired in February; no legally binding successor | FACT | New START expired on 5 February 2026. The UN Secretary-General stated that the world was left without binding limits on the US and Russian strategic arsenals and called for a successor framework. SIPRI records expiration without a follow-on treaty. |
| May NPT Review Conference ended without consensus for the third consecutive time | FACT | UN meeting coverage dated 22 May 2026 states that the Conference ended without a consensus outcome and that this was the third consecutive such failure, after 2015 and 2022. |

| SIPRI estimate of 12,187 nuclear weapons | ATTRIBUTED ESTIMATE, January 2026 | SIPRI Yearbook 2026 estimates a total global inventory of 12,187. The speech names SIPRI and says “estimates”; it does not present the figure as an official UN count. |

## CORRECTIONS FROM THE PREVIOUS GSL

The following vulnerable or inaccurate formulations were removed:

1. **Removed the formal greeting** — per delegate instruction; no “Honourable Chair” or “distinguished delegates”.
2. **Removed “this chamber has gathered for eighty-one years”** — Hiroshima and Nagasaki are at their 81st anniversaries, but the first UN General Assembly session opened in 1946.
3. **Removed “two hundred thousand dead” as an unqualified exact number** — replaced with an attributed combined municipal estimate above 200,000 by the end of 1945.
4. **Removed “fewer survivors than ever”** — replaced with the dated, checkable average age of certified hibakusha: 86.66.
5. **Removed “No negotiations”** — too time-sensitive and vulnerable to a last-minute diplomatic contact; “no legally binding successor” is the safe legal fact.
6. **Removed “one wire from the point of no return”** — speculative and dependent on a changing grid configuration. The replacement tracks the IAEA’s current 30 July language on military activity near ZNPP.
7. **Removed “the fastest buildup since the Cold War”** — unnecessary comparative claim. The global total is now directly attributed to SIPRI.
8. **Removed the rounded “twelve thousand warheads”** — replaced with SIPRI’s current 12,187 estimate and explicit attribution.
9. **Corrected the DPRK constitutional claim** — the Constitution enshrines nuclear policy; the speech does not claim that the quoted word “irreversible” appears in the constitutional text.
10. **Removed the claim that global arsenals grew every year** — historically false because total inventories declined substantially after the Cold War.
11. **Removed “the world promised Nagasaki last”** — rhetorical overstatement. The replacement is a conditional appeal, not a claim of a formal world promise.
12. **Replaced “poisoned fishermen who were never told”** — the new line uses the verified facts: all 23 were irradiated while outside the declared danger zone.
13. **Removed “vote with us”** — avoids revealing or implying a bloc. Japan speaks only for Japan and for the proposed machinery.
14. **Removed the reference to a future real-world UN meeting** — the MUN simulation is treated as the operative United Nations.
15. **Removed the quotation from Japan’s Prime Minister** — Japan now owns the Nagasaki line directly rather than quoting itself.

## POINT-OF-ORDER FIREWALL

**If challenged on the death toll:**  
“Japan expressly called the figure a municipal estimate. Hiroshima estimates approximately 140,000 deaths by the end of December 1945; Nagasaki’s official historical estimate is 73,884. Japan did not claim an exact count.”

**If challenged on the hibakusha average:**  
“Japan referred to certified hibakusha. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare reported an average age of 86.66 as of the end of March 2026; ‘nearly eighty-seven’ is accurate rounding.”

**If challenged on New START:**  
“New START expired on 5 February 2026. Japan said only that there is no legally binding successor. Any political statement, informal restraint or proposed negotiation is not a successor treaty.”

**If challenged on the NPT:**  
“Japan did not say the NPT ceased to exist. Japan said the May 2026 Review Conference ended without a consensus outcome, exactly as recorded by the United Nations, for the third consecutive Review Conference.”

**If challenged on the nuclear plant:**  
“Japan made no accident prediction and assigned no disputed strike. IAEA Update 360, dated 30 July 2026, reports increased military activity near Europe’s largest nuclear power plant and continuing safety and security challenges.”

**If challenged on 12,187:**  
“Japan explicitly attributed the number to SIPRI and explicitly called it an estimate. It is SIPRI’s January 2026 total inventory, not an official national or United Nations declaration.”

**If challenged on the DPRK Constitution:**  
“Japan said nuclear policy was placed in the Constitution. The September 2023 amendment did exactly that. Japan did not claim that the word ‘irreversible’ appears in the constitutional text.”

**If challenged on missile overflights:**  
“Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs records ballistic missiles flying over Japan, including on 4 October 2022; two Hwasong-12 missiles also overflew Japan in 2017.”

**If challenged on the four NO votes:**  
“The public UNGA record across the 2023, 2024 and 2025 nuclear-legacy texts shows the same four NO votes: France, the DPRK, Russia and the United Kingdom. Japan did not claim that every text had an identical title.”

**If challenged on Lucky Dragon:**  
“The Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum records that Lucky Dragon was well outside the declared US danger area and that all 23 crew were exposed to highly radioactive fallout.”

**If accused of exposing the bloc:**  
“Japan named no bloc, sponsor group or partner. ‘Machinery’ describes Japan’s policy proposal only.”
