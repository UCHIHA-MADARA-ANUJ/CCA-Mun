**Agenda:** Assessing the ecological threats of nuclear proliferation on global boundaries  
**Committee roster used:** 35 named portfolios, including Japan

---

## 1. HOW TO GET FULL POI MARKS

A high-scoring POI is not the longest or loudest question. It does four things:

1. **Uses one verified fact** from the target’s own record.
2. **Creates one dilemma** that cannot be escaped with a generic speech.
3. **Returns directly to the ecological agenda.**
4. **Ends within 12–18 seconds.**

### Winning formula

> “Given **[one fact]**, will the delegation support **[one concrete standard]**, or explain **[one contradiction]**?”

### Floor rules

- Ask **one question**, not a miniature speech.
- Do not say “Don’t you think…?”
- Do not put three statistics into one POI.
- If the speaker already answered one question in the GSL, do not ask it blindly. Select the POI whose trigger matches what the delegate actually said.
- Echo one phrase from the speaker before asking: “The delegate emphasized national security…” This proves active listening.
- Friendly POIs should be coordinated privately where possible. A strong delegate who gives an ally a useful answer scores diplomacy marks too.
- Ask approximately **four to six excellent POIs across the session**, not twenty weak ones.
- After asking, sit down immediately. Do not argue while the delegate answers.
- A follow-up is used only if the answer is visibly evasive and the dais permits it.

### Tone labels

- **FRIENDLY LOB:** lets an ally introduce evidence that helps Japan.
- **BRIDGE TEST:** moves a swing State toward Japan’s framework.
- **ACCOUNTABILITY TRAP:** exposes an armed State’s contradiction.
- **COUNTER ONLY:** use only if that delegation attacks Japan first.

**Best scoring balance:** two hostile traps + two swing questions + two friendly lobs.

# 3. COUNTRY CARDS

## AUSTRIA — FRIENDLY TECHNICAL LOB

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “As host to both IAEA and CTBTO institutions, does Austria support connecting existing monitoring capacity to a shared transboundary-warning protocol rather than creating another bureaucracy?”

**POI 2 — Backup:**

> “Would Austria support environmental reporting by every nuclear-armed State, including States outside the TPNW framework?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes the TPNW:**

> “While universal abolition remains contested, will Austria support monitoring, warning and remediation measures that can protect affected communities immediately?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes testing:**

> “Should CTBTO monitoring findings be connected more directly to national emergency-warning systems when radionuclide evidence indicates possible cross-border risk?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes accountability:**

> “Would Austria support comparable environmental reports from all possessors, even where treaty obligations differ?”

**What it wins:** Austria validates Japan’s machinery through Vienna’s existing institutions and prevents opponents from calling the proposal duplicative.

**Fact lock:** Vienna hosts the IAEA and CTBTO Preparatory Commission; Austria also hosts a CTBTO radionuclide laboratory at Seibersdorf.

## CHINA — ACCOUNTABILITY TRAP

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “China describes its arsenal as minimum deterrence. Without requesting locations or readiness, will China publish an official aggregate count so that the word ‘minimum’ can be measured?”

**POI 2 — If China attacks Fukushima:**

> “Does China accept IAEA-monitored data as the standard for transboundary radiological claims—including monitoring arrangements in which Chinese experts participate?”

**Follow-up if evasive:**

> “If not a total count, which comparable numerical category is China prepared to disclose?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes no first use:**

> “Does China agree that doctrine transparency is valuable but cannot substitute for comparable aggregate information?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes testing legacies:**

> “Will China support publishing aggregate environmental-monitoring data from former nuclear-test areas without disclosing military designs?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes victim assistance:**

> “Why did China abstain on three consecutive UNGA texts concerning victim assistance and environmental remediation?”

**What it wins:** China must either accept safe aggregate transparency or defend complete numerical opacity. The Fukushima counter makes China choose between IAEA data and its own political framing.

**Fact lock:** China publishes no official warhead total; its official policy emphasizes no first use and minimum deterrence. Chinese experts participate in the expanded IAEA monitoring arrangement concerning Fukushima water.

**Never say:** “China officially has 620.” That is a SIPRI estimate.

> “Will Singapore support a standardized public template limited to aggregate stockpiles and environmental liabilities, with all operational information expressly protected?”

**POI 2 — Backup:**

> “As a major port State, does Singapore support immediate notification of radiological incidents that could affect international shipping routes?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes data quality:**

> “Should national reports use identical definitions for deployed, reserve, retired and dismantled warheads?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes review:**

> “Would Singapore support an interactive session where States answer technical questions about otherwise public aggregate reports?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes ASEAN:**

> “Can Singapore help design an ASEAN-compatible notification standard that avoids political attribution during emergencies?”

**What it wins:** Fits Singapore’s technocratic, security-sensitive style and makes it difficult to oppose standardization.

## SOUTH AFRICA — FRIENDLY DISMANTLEMENT LOB

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “South Africa dismantled six nuclear weapons and accepted IAEA verification. What does that experience prove about whether security and verifiable accountability can coexist?”

**POI 2 — Backup:**

> “Should current possessors be expected to meet at least South Africa’s standard of declaring dismantlement and accepting verification?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes universality:**

> “Should non-NPT possessors receive a parallel voluntary route to meet the same aggregate standard?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes African capacity:**

> “Would South Africa support regional laboratories and training so African States can verify radiological claims independently?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes the TPNW:**

> “How can dismantlement verification and victim assistance be advanced now without waiting for universal TPNW membership?”

**What it wins:** South Africa becomes living proof that nuclear secrecy is a policy choice, not an unavoidable condition.

**Fact lock:** South Africa built six completed nuclear devices, dismantled them and submitted the former programme to IAEA verification before and after joining the NPT.

## SPAIN — ACCOUNTABILITY TRAP / LEGACY QUESTION

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “Palomares proved that a nuclear weapon need not detonate to contaminate land. Does Spain agree accountability must cover transport accidents and residual cleanup, not only nuclear tests?”

**POI 2 — Backup:**

> “Will Spain support public reporting of contaminated sites, remediation progress and cleanup timelines?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes NATO:**

> “Does Palomares show that alliance arrangements do not remove the need for public environmental accountability?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes the Mediterranean:**

> “Would Spain support a Mediterranean warning system for airborne and marine radiological pathways?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes transport:**

> “Should nuclear-weapon transport accidents trigger mandatory notification even when no nuclear detonation occurs?”

**What it wins:** This is one of the committee’s most distinctive ecological POIs and directly expands the agenda beyond detonations.

**Fact lock:** In 1966, a US bomber accident near Palomares, Spain involved four thermonuclear weapons; conventional explosions in two weapons dispersed plutonium contamination. Cleanup and monitoring followed, and Spain and the US signed a 2015 statement of intent concerning further remediation.

**Never say:** A nuclear explosion occurred at Palomares. It did not.

**POI 2 — Backup:**

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes review:**

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes institutions:**

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes finance:**

**What it wins:** Gives Switzerland ownership of the data standard and interactive accountability mechanism.

## UNITED ARAB EMIRATES — FRIENDLY PEACEFUL-USE LOB

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “As an NPDI member and operator of safeguarded civil nuclear energy, will the UAE support standardized military reporting while protecting peaceful-use rights?”

**POI 2 — Backup:**

> “How can transparent safeguards for civil nuclear energy help this committee distinguish peaceful programmes from military stockpiles?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes regional safety:**

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes funding:**

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes non-NPT States:**

> “Should non-NPT possessors be invited into a parallel reporting track so universality does not depend on treaty status?”

**What it wins:** Makes the civil–military firewall credible and recruits a technologically capable Gulf State.

**Fact lock:** The UAE is an NPDI member; the Barakah programme operates under the UAE’s non-proliferation commitments and IAEA safeguards.

## UNITED KINGDOM — ACCOUNTABILITY TRAP

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “The United Kingdom publishes a ceiling of 260 but withholds operational categories. Why can it not publish aggregate deployed, reserve and retired figures without revealing locations?”

**POI 2 — Backup:**

> “The UK opposed three consecutive nuclear-legacy texts. Does it oppose remediation itself, or what specific alternative does it propose?”

**Follow-up if it cites security:**

> “Norway and the UK themselves researched information barriers. Why can that principle not protect aggregate reporting?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes the UK–Norway Initiative:**

> “If information barriers can protect warhead data during verification research, why can they not support more transparent aggregate reporting?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes test legacies:**

> “Will the UK release environmental and historical testing data requested by affected former test territories?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes dismantlement:**

> “Will the UK publish annual aggregate dismantlement figures without revealing operational deployments?”

**What it wins:** Uses the UK’s own ceiling and Norway partnership against a blanket national-security defence.

**Fact lock:** The UK publishes an overall ceiling of no more than 260 but withholds operational-stockpile, deployed-warhead and deployed-missile numbers.

## UNITED STATES — ACCOUNTABILITY TEST

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

> “The United States officially disclosed 3,748 stockpile warheads as of September 2023. Will it support comparable current reports from every nuclear possessor?”

**POI 2 — Ecological accountability:**

> “US auditors report remaining Energy Department cleanup above half a trillion dollars. Will the US support environmental liabilities as part of nuclear-stockpile accountability?”

**POI 3 — If the speaker emphasizes New START:**

> “Will the United States publish annual aggregate categories until a legally binding successor restores reciprocal reporting?”

**POI 4 — If the speaker emphasizes testing legacies:**

> “Will the United States support a uniform standard for testing archives, contaminated sites, cleanup funding and timelines?”

**POI 5 — If the speaker emphasizes victim assistance:**

> “Why did the United States abstain on three consecutive UNGA nuclear-legacy texts, and what alternative assistance mechanism does it propose?”

**What it wins:** Praises genuine US transparency before demanding reciprocity. The ecological question uses the US government’s own audit rather than an adversary’s allegation.

**Fact lock:** The official US figure is 3,748 active and inactive stockpile warheads as of September 2023—not the SIPRI total inventory. GAO reported that DOE estimated remaining Environmental Management cleanup above half a trillion dollars.

# 4. JAPAN — QUESTIONS YOU WILL RECEIVE AND PERFECT ANSWERS

## “Japan has 44.4 tonnes of plutonium. How can it demand transparency?”

> “Correct. Japan publicly reports approximately 44.4 tonnes of separated civilian plutonium, including domestic and overseas holdings. It is civilian material, reported annually and subject to safeguards—not an undeclared warhead stockpile. Japan asks possessors to meet the same aggregate-accountability standard.”

**Do not use the stale 45.1-tonne figure in older notes. The current official end-2024 figure is approximately 44.4 tonnes.**

## “What about Fukushima ALPS-treated water?”

> “Japan accepts IAEA monitoring, publishes the data and includes laboratories from concerned neighbouring States. That is exactly Japan’s principle: disputed radiological claims should be resolved through shared monitoring, not national assurances alone.”

## “Japan relies on the US nuclear umbrella.”

> “Extended deterrence is a security arrangement; environmental accountability is a universal rule. Japan applies that rule to allies and competitors alike.”

## “Why has Japan not joined the TPNW?”

> “Japan works through the NPT framework because it includes nuclear-weapon and non-nuclear-weapon States. This committee need not settle that treaty debate to agree on monitoring, warning and remediation now.”

## “Can the IAEA count every warhead under your proposal?”

> “No. Ordinary IAEA safeguards do not authorize warhead counting. Japan proposes public aggregate reporting first, protected exchanges second and negotiated verification only with an agreed mandate.”

## “Your framework is voluntary and therefore toothless.”

> “Voluntary aggregate reporting creates categories, baselines and political accountability. A compulsory mechanism this Assembly cannot legally impose would be performative, not stronger.”

## “You are politicizing Zaporizhzhya.”

> “Japan’s wording names no State and predicts no accident. It follows current IAEA language on military activity and nuclear-safety risks near the plant.”

## “Why use SIPRI instead of official counts?”

> “Because no universal official register exists. Japan labels every SIPRI number as an estimate. Any possessor may correct the estimate by publishing an official aggregate total.”

# 5. LIVE SELECTION MAP

## If the delegate speaks about “national security”

Ask: China, France, India, Israel, Russia, UK.

> “Which operational secret would be exposed by aggregate environmental reporting?”

## If the delegate speaks about “sovereignty”

Ask: Russia, DPRK, Israel, Iran.

> “Does sovereignty include the right to externalize radiological risk without notification?”

## If the delegate speaks about “verification”

Ask: Austria, Brazil, Norway, South Africa, Switzerland.

Use a friendly technical lob and let that State validate Japan’s machinery.

## If the delegate attacks Fukushima

Use the China counter format regardless of speaker:

> “Does the delegation accept IAEA-monitored and internationally participated data as the standard—yes or no?”

## If the delegate demands abolition only

Ask:

> “While abolition remains contested, does the delegation oppose monitoring, warning or remediation that can protect people immediately?”

## If the delegate says the issue is historical

Ask:

> “If the consequences remain in soil, groundwater and human bodies, on what date did they become merely historical?”

## If the delegate says transparency is not disarmament

Ask:

> “Agreed. But how can any reduction be verified if States cannot first agree on what categories must be reported?”

# 6. NEVER-USE POIS

Do not ask any of the following:

- “How many bombs can Japan make from its plutonium?” — misuse of safeguards quantities.
- “Why does Iran have nuclear weapons?” — Iran is not an established nuclear-armed State.
- “Why does China officially have 620 warheads?” — 620 is an estimate, not an official Chinese count.
- “Why does North Korea have exactly 60 warheads?” — estimates are uncertain.
- “Why can the IAEA not inspect your warheads tomorrow?” — ordinary safeguards provide no such mandate.
- “Why has the US refused to clean every nuclear site?” — overbroad and unsupported.
- “Did Palomares experience a nuclear explosion?” — it did not.
- “Does every nuclear accident cross borders?” — not necessarily; say “could”.
- “Why has the UN done absolutely nothing for 80 years?” — factually indefensible.
- Any POI that names VANGUARD, its members or coordinated clause ownership before the sponsor reveal.

## FINAL DOCTRINE

The aim is not to embarrass every delegation. The aim is to make the dais notice that Japan can:

- expose an inconsistency without misquoting a State;
- help an ally contribute substance;
- distinguish estimates from official figures;
- protect legitimate security information;
- return every answer to monitoring, warning and remediation.

That combination—not aggression alone—is what earns full POI marks.
