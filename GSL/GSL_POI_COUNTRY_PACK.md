# GSL — MAXIMUM-AGGRESSION COUNTRY-BY-COUNTRY POI ARSENAL

## Delegation of Japan · CCA MUN ’26 · UNGA

**Agenda:** Assessing the ecological threats of nuclear proliferation on global boundaries

**Coverage:** Five original, country-specific POIs for each of 34 other delegations

**Total:** 170 GSL POIs + Japan’s incoming-POI defence + rapid live-trigger questions

**Design:** One verified premise, one contradiction and one answerable demand—without copied generic endings

**Status:** Final maximum-aggression, fact-screened pack · 22 August 2026

---

## HOW TO USE THIS PACK

- Ask the question that answers the delegate’s actual GSL; never mechanically use POI 1.
- Aggression comes from **precision**, not unsupported accusations. Make the delegate choose between two defensible options and own the choice.
- Use approximately four to six high-value POIs across the GSL session: two hostile traps, two swing-State tests and two friendly technical lobs.
- For an ally, ask a question that lets it introduce evidence or machinery Japan needs. For an opponent, expose the exact missing record, mechanism or contribution.
- If the dais requires written POIs, submit the quotation exactly. If asked orally, speak slowly and stop after the question mark.
- Never argue over the answer unless a follow-up is explicitly permitted.
- Japan’s public doctrine is universal: **same ecological standard for ally and adversary; legitimate secrets protected; environmental consequences not erased.**

### Priority attack order

1. France — ended numerical arsenal disclosure after previously proving it was possible

7. Israel — strategic ambiguity versus emergency and environmental transparency
8. Spain — Palomares proves contamination does not require a nuclear detonation

**POI 1 — Best overall:**

# NEVER-USE GSL POIS

- “Why does Iran possess nuclear weapons?” — Iran is not an established nuclear-armed State.
- “Why does China officially have [estimated number] warheads?” — external estimates are not official Chinese totals.
- “How many warheads does Israel officially possess?” — Israel maintains ambiguity and declares no official total.
- “Why does Norway host nuclear weapons?” — do not claim Norway hosts them.
- “Can the IAEA inspect every warhead tomorrow?” — ordinary safeguards provide no such authority.
- “Can CTBTO conduct treaty on-site inspections now?” — not before CTBT entry into force.
- “Did Palomares experience a nuclear explosion?” — it did not.
- “Is Runit Dome cracking and leaking?” — official evidence requires a more nuanced formulation.
- “Did every nuclear test cross an international border?” — not established.
- “Is every part of the Kazakhstan site dangerous?” — contamination is heterogeneous.
- “Does every nuclear accident cause transboundary contamination?” — not necessarily.
- “Does assistance prove liability?” — no.
- “Is NPT Article IV the safeguards article?” — Article III covers safeguards; Article IV protects peaceful use.
- “Does TPNW bind nuclear-armed States?” — none is currently a party.
- Any POI presenting SIPRI, FAS or another external estimate as an official national declaration.
- Any question revealing Japan’s private coordination, speaker order, sponsor strategy or vote count.
